\subsection{Physical costs and validation}
\input{generated/study2_table_ppa}

Higher frequency comes with a resource trade-off. On the
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaCommonSupport}-design common support, RF uses
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfLut} rather than
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftLut} LUTs and
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfFf} rather than
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftFf} flip-flops.  Conversely, DSP use falls from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftDsp} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfDsp} blocks, while BRAM remains unchanged.
Vectorless power rises from \studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftPower} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfPower}~W.  The paired intervals in
Table~\ref{tab:study2-ppa} show that the LUT, register, DSP, and vectorless-power
directions are stable on common support.  These conditional quantities answer
what a compiled correct implementation costs; they do not replace the
failure-penalized policy endpoint.

\input{generated/study2_table_rtl_case}

The RTL pair in Table~\ref{tab:study2-rtl-case} illustrates this trade-off.  For \studyclaim{StudyTwoCaseDesign}, the dominant SFT
candidate stores delayed samples and products before a wide parallel sum.  The
dominant candidate from RF seed one instead emits a registered transposed
multiply--accumulate chain.  Vivado maps the latter to substantially more LUTs
and registers, no DSP blocks, and a much shorter timing path.  Because this
case was selected post-primary for interpretability, it illustrates
a physically meaningful RTL transformation but does not establish that every
family or every gain follows the same transformation.

Physical sensitivity checks support relative agreement with limits on absolute
frequency. The selected setup-closure pilot gives Spearman correlation
\revisionclaim{ClosureRho}, median symmetric error
\revisionclaim{ClosureMedianSmape}\%, and no reversal among
\revisionclaim{ClosureNonTiedPairs} non-tied pairs. Its worst candidate error is
\revisionclaim{ClosureWorstSmape}\%, on reverse FIR. Separately, the cross-version
coverage diagnostic confirms a setup path and clear constraint counts for
\revisionclaim{CoverageValid} of \revisionclaim{CoverageCandidates} candidates;
\revisionclaim{CoverageInvalid} lacks a path and
\revisionclaim{CoverageInconclusive} remain inconclusive. On common valid
candidates, median symmetric errors are \revisionclaim{CoverageSftMedianError}\%
for SFT, \revisionclaim{CoverageRfOneMedianError}\% for RF seed one, and
\revisionclaim{CoverageRfTwoMedianError}\% for RF seed two. The Supplementary
Material gives all cases and preserves the distinction between diagnostic
agreement and a revised primary endpoint.

The paired board measurements provide a further physical check
(Table~\ref{tab:study2-board} and Fig.~\ref{fig:board-evidence}). Across
\boardclaim{BoardRunCount} complete sweeps, the maximum
within-entry boundary spread is \boardclaim{BoardMaximumSpread}~MHz.  The echo
canary reaches \boardclaim{BoardCanaryFmax}~MHz and remains at least
\boardclaim{BoardMinimumCanaryMargin}~MHz above every DUT boundary. The
descriptive mean changes from \boardclaim{BoardSftMean} to
\boardclaim{BoardRfMean}~MHz, a \boardclaim{BoardMeanDifference}-MHz paired
difference driven by two large feed-forward gains. Polynomial and IIR tie,
while the median pair changes by \boardclaim{BoardMedDifference}~MHz. The
board results thus support the overall physical direction but show substantial
variation across families. As a descriptive subset measured under the amended
protocol in the Supplementary Material, they do not independently confirm the
population estimate.

\subsection{Structural changes and sampling efficiency}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The sampled candidates suggest that optimization often increases the
probability of implementations already accessible to SFT. Among
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSealedMechanismCommonCount} designs with passing sampled
strings under both policies, SFT's measured support contains a
candidate at least as fast as the RF conditional mean on
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSealedMechanismSupportCount}.  Mean inverse-Simpson
effective correct-string support contracts from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSealedMechanismSftEffective} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSealedMechanismRfEffective}. Together, these observations
are consistent with a shift toward faster implementations on most designs
with common support. The samples are finite, however, and some sealed designs
fall outside this pattern, leaving circuit novelty unresolved.

On the sealed distribution, one RF draw lies between the empirical SFT
perfect-selector expectations for \revisionclaim{SelectorLowerN} draws
(\revisionclaim{SelectorLowerFmax}~MHz) and
\revisionclaim{SelectorUpperN} draws
(\revisionclaim{SelectorUpperFmax}~MHz).  At the upper integer crossing, the
SFT workload is \revisionclaim{SelectorUpperN} generations and
\revisionclaim{SelectorStreams} oracle simulation streams per design, with
\revisionclaim{SelectorRawImplementations} expected correct-draw implementation
attempts without caching or \revisionclaim{SelectorCachedImplementations}
distinct implementations with content caching.  These are empirical expected
workloads, not measured runtime or speedup.  They exclude policy-training cost
and assume perfect post-route selection on observed support; they do not imply
dominance over an unrestricted SFT search.

Source-feature aggregates give a complementary view across
\revisionclaim{StructureDesigns} designs with passing support in SFT, RF, and
correctness-only. The mean maximum multiplications per statement falls from
\revisionclaim{StructureStatementMultSft} for SFT to
\revisionclaim{StructureStatementMultRf} for RF, while correctness-only reaches
\revisionclaim{StructureStatementMultCorrectness}. RF also has higher structural
accumulation and pipeline-ratio scores. These descriptive lexical differences
are consistent with staged arithmetic and the measured FIR example. Because
some features also enter the reward, they are not independent causal evidence
or measurements of physical pipeline depth. The Supplementary Material reports
the complete comparison and its weighting.

Within the accelerator domain, the RF checkpoint trajectory retains the
physical gain from midpoint to endpoint. Interpolation changes from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoInterpMidFmax} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoInterpEndFmax}~MHz and extrapolation from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoExtrapMidFmax} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoExtrapEndFmax}~MHz. The Supplementary Material presents this
trajectory alongside the earlier reward failure, keeping the evidence from
the repaired run separate from the history that motivated it. Together with
the matched VerilogEval result, these checks show why physical gains within
the target domain and broader RTL competence should be evaluated separately.
The Supplementary Material also provides a runnable FIR example and a completed
RF versus SFT comparison on fresh draws with bounded routed setup-period
measurements. These post-primary results retain their own measurement contract
and are reported separately from the sealed primary estimate.
