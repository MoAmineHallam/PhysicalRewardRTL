\subsection{Sealed primary result}

\input{generated/study2_table_comparison}

The separately frozen study is the primary result.  Across
\studyclaim{StudyTwoDesignCount} held-out designs, the RF-reward arm raises
penalized equal-sample Fmax from \studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallSftPenalized} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfPenalized}~MHz.  The paired gain is
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfSftGain}~MHz with frozen interval
[\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfSftCiLow},
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfSftCiHigh}]~MHz.  Interpolation improves by
\studyclaim{StudyTwoInterpRfSftGain}~MHz
[\studyclaim{StudyTwoInterpRfSftCiLow},
\studyclaim{StudyTwoInterpRfSftCiHigh}], while extrapolation improves by
\studyclaim{StudyTwoExtrapRfSftGain}~MHz
[\studyclaim{StudyTwoExtrapRfSftCiLow},
\studyclaim{StudyTwoExtrapRfSftCiHigh}].

Both independently trained RF policies improve overall, by
\studyclaim{StudyTwoRfSeedOneOverallGain} and
\studyclaim{StudyTwoRfSeedTwoOverallGain}~MHz.  Functional validity remains
close rather than collapsing: correctness is
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSftCorrectness}\% for SFT and
\studyclaim{StudyTwoRfCorrectness}\% for RF.  Because incorrect and failed
samples remain zeros, this small decline is already paid for in the headline
endpoint.

The matched MLP reward improves on SFT but remains below RF.  The bounded direct
comparison gives an overall RF-minus-MLP effect of
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpGain}~MHz with interval
[\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpCiLow},
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpCiHigh}].  Its interpolation and extrapolation
intervals also exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:study2-comparison}).  A matched
correctness-only policy increases functional yield but does not reproduce the
physical endpoint gain, separating hardware-directed optimization from simply
rewarding validity.

\subsection{Conditional physical-cost trade-off}

\input{generated/study2_table_ppa}

The speed gain is not free.  On the
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaCommonSupport}-design three-arm common support, RF uses
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfLut} versus \studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftLut} LUTs and
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfFf} versus \studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftFf} flip-flops.
Conversely, it uses \studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfDsp} versus
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftDsp} DSP blocks.  Vectorless power rises from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftPower} to \studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfPower}~W.  These
figures condition on compiled correct draws and estimate tool power rather than
board power; they characterize implementation cost but do not replace the
failure-penalized primary endpoint.

\subsection{Candidate-distribution mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_mechanism_verified}
\caption{Real post-route frequency of every distinct oracle-correct candidate
in the earlier supporting evaluation.  Marker area preserves original sample
multiplicity.  GRPO concentrates probability on fewer high-frequency
implementations for the feed-forward families; recursive and median designs
show the boundary of that pattern.}
\label{fig:mechanism-verified}
\end{figure*}

Across designs, the mean all-sample mass on the most frequent distinct correct
implementation rises from \claim{MechanismSftDominantMass}\% under SFT to
\claim{MechanismGrpoDominantMass}\% under GRPO.  The effective number of correct
implementations correspondingly contracts from \claim{MechanismSftEffective}
to \claim{MechanismGrpoEffective}.  This is not simply the discovery of a
candidate absent from supervision: on \claim{MechanismSupportCount} of the
\claim{DesignCount} designs, the measured SFT support already contains a
candidate at least as fast as the GRPO conditional mean.

The median family is a useful counterexample.  Its dominant-implementation mass
falls from \claim{MechanismMedianSftDominantMass}\% to
\claim{MechanismMedianGrpoDominantMass}\% instead of concentrating.  Together
with Table~\ref{tab:family-verified}, this supports a distribution-reallocation
mechanism for the large gains without claiming that every family follows it or
that textual identity proves a shared microarchitecture.

\subsection{Sampling efficiency}

\input{generated/table_bestofn}

One optimized draw reaches the empirical perfect-selector SFT curve at
approximately \claim{InterpEquivalentDraws} supervised draws under
interpolation and \claim{ExtrapEquivalentDraws} under extrapolation.  It does
not exceed the perfect selector at the largest evaluated budget:
interpolation gives \claim{InterpSftBestofFortyeight}~MHz for supervised

dominance over arbitrary best-of-$N$ budgets.

\subsection{Contextual baselines and general-RTL control}

\input{generated/table_context}

The stored API baseline illustrates why conditional speed alone is
insufficient.  An explicit timing prompt raises conditional frequency from
\claim{FrontierPlainConditional} to \claim{FrontierFastConditional}~MHz, but
correctness changes from \claim{FrontierPlainCorrectness}\% to
\claim{FrontierFastCorrectness}\%.  Its penalized result is therefore only
\claim{FrontierFastPenalized}~MHz, versus \claim{OverallGrpoPenalized}~MHz for
GRPO.  Because this is one API configuration with fewer samples, it establishes
a same-task reference point rather than a broad superiority claim.

The HLS comparison asks a different question: how good is a selected fast RTL
implementation?  Across \claim{HlsDesignCount} designs with post-implementation
HLS results, the fastest measured GRPO candidate reaches
\claim{HlsExpertGeomean}$\times$ the throughput of engineer-pragma HLS in
geometric mean.  Against no-pragma HLS it reaches
\claim{HlsNaiveGeomean}$\times$, ranging from
\claim{HlsNaiveMinimum}$\times$ to \claim{HlsNaiveMaximum}$\times$.  The RTL
column is selected rather than policy-average, and HLS starts from engineer-
written C++; these values bound implementation quality but are not the primary
causal comparison.

On \claim{VerilogProblemCount} VerilogEval problems with
\claim{VerilogSamplesPerProblem} samples each, pass-at-one falls from
\claim{VerilogBasePassone}\% for the base model to
\claim{VerilogSftPassone}\% after domain specialization.  GRPO reaches
\claim{VerilogGrpoPassone}\%, a change of \claim{VerilogRlDelta} percentage
points from SFT.  Thus specialization has a substantial general-RTL cost, but
physical policy optimization adds no further measured regression.  SFT and GRPO
also have the same \claim{VerilogSftCompileFail}\% compile-failure rate.

\subsection{Bounded reward-overoptimization diagnostic}
\label{sec:trajectory}

\input{generated/table_trajectory}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_trajectory_verified}
\caption{Training proxy and real Vivado measurements on the fixed FIR/reverse-
FIR trajectory subset.  The proxy diagnoses optimization and is never used as
an evaluation result.}
\label{fig:trajectory-verified}
\end{figure}

Early optimization is real: from the starting to the early checkpoint,
penalized measured frequency rises from \claim{TrajectoryStartPenalized} to
\claim{TrajectoryEarlyPenalized}~MHz.  The late phase differs.  Proxy reward
rises from \claim{TrajectoryLateProxy} to
\claim{TrajectoryFinalProxy}~MHz, while conditional measured frequency changes
only from \claim{TrajectoryLateConditional} to
\claim{TrajectoryFinalConditional}~MHz.  Correctness falls from
\claim{TrajectoryLateCorrectness}\% to
\claim{TrajectoryFinalCorrectness}\%, so penalized performance falls from
\claim{TrajectoryLatePenalized} to \claim{TrajectoryFinalPenalized}~MHz.

A source audit identifies a plausible lexical channel.  The deployed feature
counts nonblocking assignments that begin a line.  Between the late and final
checkpoints, its multiplicity-weighted value rises from
\claim{ReflowLateRawNonblocking} to
\claim{ReflowFinalRawNonblocking}, even as token count falls from
\claim{ReflowLateTokens} to \claim{ReflowFinalTokens}.  Under a diagnostic
layout canonicalization, the assignment count instead changes from
\claim{ReflowLateCanonicalNonblocking} to
\claim{ReflowFinalCanonicalNonblocking}, and canonical rescoring removes
\claim{ReflowRemoved}\% of the raw late reward jump.  The canonical scores are
themselves clamp-saturated, and the search found only
\claim{ReflowTokenPairCount} token-identical cross-checkpoint pairs.  We
therefore treat this as bounded attribution evidence, not a validated repaired
reward or assumption-free causal proof.
