\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Primary equal-sample result}

\input{generated/table_main}

Table~\ref{tab:main-verified} is the paper's primary result.  Under interpolation,
penalized frequency rises from \claim{InterpSftPenalized} to
\claim{InterpGrpoPenalized}~MHz, a paired gain of \claim{InterpGain}~MHz.  Under
extrapolation it rises from \claim{ExtrapSftPenalized} to
\claim{ExtrapGrpoPenalized}~MHz, a paired gain of \claim{ExtrapGain}~MHz.  The
bootstrap intervals remain above zero in both regimes.  Overall,
\claim{OverallImproved} designs improve and \claim{OverallDeclined} decline.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_main_verified}
\caption{Per-design primary endpoint.  Each vertical segment joins supervised
and optimized results for one frozen held-out design.}
\label{fig:main-verified}
\end{figure}

The result is not obtained by discarding functional failures.  Supervised and
optimized correctness are \claim{OverallSftCorrectness}\% and
\claim{OverallGrpoCorrectness}\% overall.  Interpolation correctness is nearly
flat, moving from \claim{InterpSftCorrectness}\% to
\claim{InterpGrpoCorrectness}\%; extrapolation correctness falls from
\claim{ExtrapSftCorrectness}\% to \claim{ExtrapGrpoCorrectness}\%.  Because the
primary metric already scores those failures as zero, the frequency improvement
survives this reliability cost rather than hiding it.

\subsection{Variation across families}

\input{generated/table_family}

Table~\ref{tab:family-verified} shows that the aggregate improvement is broad but
not uniform.  FIR, reverse-FIR, and polynomial designs provide the largest
effects.  Recursive and median designs are weaker, which is consistent with the
candidate inspection: their supervised distributions contain less separable
fast alternatives for policy optimization to amplify.  This heterogeneity is a
boundary on the method, not a reason to average those designs away.

\subsection{Sampling efficiency}

\input{generated/table_bestofn}

One optimized draw reaches the empirical perfect-selector SFT curve at
approximately \claim{InterpEquivalentDraws} supervised draws under interpolation
and \claim{ExtrapEquivalentDraws} under extrapolation.  It does not exceed the
perfect selector at its largest evaluated budget: interpolation gives
\claim{InterpSftBestofFortyeight}~MHz for supervised sampling against
\claim{InterpGrpoOne}~MHz for one optimized draw, and extrapolation gives
\claim{ExtrapSftBestofFortyeight} against \claim{ExtrapGrpoOne}~MHz.  The
defensible claim is therefore sample efficiency, not dominance over every large
best-of-$N$ budget.

\subsection{Bounded reward-overoptimization diagnostic}
\label{sec:trajectory}

\input{generated/table_trajectory}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_trajectory_verified}
\caption{Training proxy and real Vivado measurements on the fixed FIR/FIRR
trajectory subset.  The proxy is shown only to diagnose optimization and is not
used as a paper result.}
\label{fig:trajectory-verified}
\end{figure}

Early optimization is real: from the starting checkpoint to the early
checkpoint, penalized measured frequency rises from
\claim{TrajectoryStartPenalized} to \claim{TrajectoryEarlyPenalized}~MHz.  The
late phase differs.  Between the late and final checkpoints, proxy reward rises
from \claim{TrajectoryLateProxy} to \claim{TrajectoryFinalProxy}~MHz, while
conditional measured frequency moves only from
\claim{TrajectoryLateConditional} to \claim{TrajectoryFinalConditional}~MHz.
Correctness falls from \claim{TrajectoryLateCorrectness}\% to
\claim{TrajectoryFinalCorrectness}\%, so penalized measured performance falls
from \claim{TrajectoryLatePenalized} to
\claim{TrajectoryFinalPenalized}~MHz.

This supports a precise and limited conclusion: the deployed proxy became
misaligned with measured performance late in this run.  It does not establish
that every proxy architecture fails, that clamp saturation removes all gradient,
or that saturation alone predicts correctness loss.
