\subsection{Reward-package controls and endpoint decomposition}

\input{generated/revision_table_decomposition}

Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} separates functional yield from the physical
quality of oracle-passing draws.  With equal budgets across designs, pooled
$q\mu$ reproduces the primary mean exactly; this pooled $\mu$ is not an
unweighted mean of per-design conditional frequencies.  RF increases pooled
conditional frequency from \revisionclaim{SftConditional} to
\revisionclaim{RfConditional}~MHz while correctness decreases.  In contrast,
the correctness-only policy has a conditional frequency of
\revisionclaim{CorrectnessConditional}~MHz.  The observed RF gain is thus a
shift in the physical quality of passing draws, not an increase in their count.

Figure~\ref{fig:study2-controls} separates physical utility from functional
yield.  Correctness-only training reaches
\studyclaim{StudyTwoCorrectnessOnlyCorrectness}\% correctness, above both SFT
and RF, but attains only
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallCorrectnessOnlyPenalized}~MHz penalized \fmax.  Its
paired SFT-relative effect is
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallCorrectnessOnlySftGain}~MHz with interval
[\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallCorrectnessOnlySftCiLow},
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallCorrectnessOnlySftCiHigh}]~MHz.  Increasing validity
alone therefore does not explain the hardware result in this realized seed.
Its interval resamples designs conditional on that checkpoint; it does not
establish a seed-robust ranking of correctness-only training.

The original MLP reward does improve over SFT, reaching
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallMlpPenalized}~MHz with a paired gain of
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallMlpSftGain}~MHz.  The repaired RF nonetheless exceeds
it directly by \studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpGain}~MHz with interval
[\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpCiLow},
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpCiHigh}]~MHz.  The RF-minus-MLP intervals remain
above zero under interpolation and extrapolation as well.  Because the arms
share their SFT initialization, tasks, update budget, oracle, and evaluation,
this comparison favors the RF reward package for these checkpoints.  It does
not separate canonicalization, structural features, and predictor architecture,
nor does it compare alternative policy-optimization algorithms.

\subsection{Conditional physical-cost trade-off}

\input{generated/study2_table_ppa}

The speed improvement is not a free PPA gain.  On the
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaCommonSupport}-design common support, RF uses
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfLut} rather than
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftLut} LUTs and
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfFf} rather than
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftFf} flip-flops.  Conversely, DSP use falls from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftDsp} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfDsp} blocks, while BRAM remains unchanged.
Vectorless power rises from \studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaSftPower} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoPpaRfPower}~W.  The paired intervals in
Table~\ref{tab:study2-ppa} show that the LUT, register, DSP, and vectorless-power
directions are stable on common support.  These conditional quantities answer
what a compiled correct implementation costs; they do not replace the
failure-penalized policy endpoint.

\input{generated/study2_table_rtl_case}

Table~\ref{tab:study2-rtl-case} supplies a concrete CAD-level example of that
aggregate trade-off.  For \studyclaim{StudyTwoCaseDesign}, the dominant SFT
candidate stores delayed samples and products before a wide parallel sum.  The
dominant candidate from RF seed one instead emits a registered transposed
multiply--accumulate chain.  Vivado maps the latter to substantially more LUTs
and registers, no DSP blocks, and a much shorter timing path.  Because this
case was selected post-primary for interpretability, it illustrates
a physically meaningful RTL transformation but does not establish that every
family or every gain follows the same transformation.

\subsection{Setup-closure sensitivity}

\input{generated/revision_table_closure}

The selected-candidate pilot yields Spearman correlation
\revisionclaim{ClosureRho} between original timing-derived and setup-passing
frequencies, with median symmetric percentage error
\revisionclaim{ClosureMedianSmape}\%.  None of the
\revisionclaim{ClosureNonTiedPairs} non-tied pairs reverses direction

\subsection{Breadth and backbone transfer}

The earlier, separate \claim{DesignCount}-design evaluation supplies broader
five-family support.  Its penalized endpoint rises from
\claim{OverallSftPenalized} to \claim{OverallGrpoPenalized}~MHz, with
\claim{OverallImproved} of \claim{DesignCount} designs improving.  Feed-forward

\claim{QwenOverallSftCorrectness}\% to

explanation, but its narrower family universe prevents direct pooling.

\subsection{Probability reallocation and sampling efficiency}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Repeating the support diagnostic on the sealed samples refines the mechanism
claim.  Among \studyclaim{StudyTwoSealedMechanismCommonCount} designs with
correct sampled strings under both policies, SFT's measured support contains a
candidate at least as fast as the RF conditional mean on
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSealedMechanismSupportCount}.  Mean inverse-Simpson
effective correct-string support contracts from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSealedMechanismSftEffective} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSealedMechanismRfEffective}.  Thus reallocation explains
most common-support cases, but the sealed exceptions prevent the stronger claim
that every optimized implementation was already observed in the finite SFT
sample.  Nor do unseen SFT samples permit a claim of open-ended invention.

On the sealed distribution, one RF draw lies between the empirical SFT
perfect-selector expectations for \revisionclaim{SelectorLowerN} draws
(\revisionclaim{SelectorLowerFmax}~MHz) and
\revisionclaim{SelectorUpperN} draws
(\revisionclaim{SelectorUpperFmax}~MHz).  At the upper integer crossing, the
SFT workload is \revisionclaim{SelectorUpperN} generations and
\revisionclaim{SelectorStreams} oracle simulation streams per design, with
\revisionclaim{SelectorRawImplementations} expected correct-draw implementation
attempts without caching or \revisionclaim{SelectorCachedImplementations}
distinct implementations with content caching.  These are empirical expected
workloads, not measured runtime or speedup.  They exclude policy-training cost
and assume perfect post-route selection on observed support; they do not imply
dominance over an unrestricted SFT search.

\subsection{Contextual bounds}

The same-task API control shows why conditional speed is insufficient.  A
timing-focused prompt raises conditional \fmax{} from
\claim{FrontierPlainConditional} to
\claim{FrontierFastConditional}~MHz while correctness changes from
\claim{FrontierPlainCorrectness}\% to
\claim{FrontierFastCorrectness}\%; its penalized endpoint is
\claim{FrontierFastPenalized}~MHz.  This is one API configuration with a smaller
budget and is not a general frontier-model ranking.

For selected implementation quality, the fastest measured optimized RTL
candidate reaches \claim{HlsExpertGeomean}$\times$ the geometric-mean
throughput of engineer-pragma HLS over \claim{HlsDesignCount} comparable
designs.  It reaches \claim{HlsNaiveGeomean}$\times$ no-pragma HLS.  Because HLS
starts from engineer-written C++ and the RTL value is selected rather than
policy-average, this comparison is context, not the causal baseline.

\subsection{Bounded reward-reliability diagnostic}
\label{sec:trajectory}

Figure~\ref{fig:reward-reliability} contrasts the older exploited reward with
the repaired RF trajectory.  In the historical run, early penalized physical
performance rises from \claim{TrajectoryStartPenalized} to
\claim{TrajectoryEarlyPenalized}~MHz.  Late in training, however, the proxy
rises from \claim{TrajectoryLateProxy} to
\claim{TrajectoryFinalProxy}~MHz while correctness falls from
\claim{TrajectoryLateCorrectness}\% to
\claim{TrajectoryFinalCorrectness}\%.  Penalized physical performance
therefore falls from \claim{TrajectoryLatePenalized} to
\claim{TrajectoryFinalPenalized}~MHz.

A lexical audit identifies one plausible channel in the older reward: a
line-anchored assignment feature increases while its layout-canonical
counterpart does not, and diagnostic rescoring removes
\claim{ReflowRemoved}\% of the raw late reward jump.  The canonical scores are
clamp-saturated and there are \claim{ReflowTokenPairCount} token-identical
cross-checkpoint pairs, so this is bounded attribution rather than a randomized
causal test.  In the repaired run, interpolation remains essentially stable
from midpoint to endpoint and extrapolation improves.  The positive repair
claim comes from the sealed efficacy study; the historical trajectory only
explains why the reporting boundary is necessary.
