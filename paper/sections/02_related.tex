\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Executable RTL generation}

VerilogEval and RTLLM established simulation-backed evaluation for
natural-language-to-RTL generation~\cite{liu2023verilogeval,lu2024rtllm}.
VeriGen, RTLCoder, CodeV, and CraftRTL improve generation through specialized
pretraining, supervised adaptation, data construction, or targeted repair
~\cite{thakur2024verigen,liu2024rtlcoder,zhao2024codev,liu2024craftrtl}.
VeriSeek introduces code-structure-guided reinforcement learning, while
VeriReason combines supervised learning, testbench feedback, and group-relative
policy optimization~\cite{wang2024veriseek,wang2025verireason}.  FormalRTL
couples executable specifications to formal verification in a multi-agent
synthesis flow~\cite{formalrtl2026}.  These systems make correctness an
executable outcome.  Our oracle serves the same indispensable role, but our
dependent variable is the physical quality of the entire sampled policy
distribution after correctness has been enforced.

\subsection{LLM-driven physical optimization}

Physical feedback enters current RTL workflows at several different loci.
RTLRewriter rewrites an existing implementation~\cite{yao2024rtlrewriter}.
PPA-RTL constructs synthesis-scored preference pairs and applies direct
preference optimization (DPO)~\cite{ppartl2025}.  ChipSeek uses curriculum-guided
dynamic policy optimization with syntax, functional, and synthesis-derived
feedback~\cite{chipseek2025}.  Alpha-RTL instead adapts the
policy for each target at test time through an executable EDA loop
~\cite{zhou2026alphartl}.  Dr.~RTL uses commercial-tool feedback, critical-path
analysis, parallel rewriting, and reusable optimization skills
~\cite{drrtl2026}.  POET and EvolVE apply evolutionary search to verified RTL
variants~\cite{poet2026,evolve2026}.  RTL-OPT contributes a benchmark of
suboptimal/human-optimized RTL pairs and an automated correctness/PPA
evaluation flow~\cite{lu2026rtlopt}.

These approaches answer related but non-identical questions.  Per-design
adaptation, evolutionary search, and rewriting naturally evaluate the selected
implementation returned by the search.  Training before deployment asks whether
the generator itself has improved before any post-generation selection.  Our
primary object is therefore the equal-draw empirical policy distribution:
sample multiplicity, incorrect outputs, and implementation failures all remain
in the denominator.  The perfect-selector comparison separately gives
inference-time search the most favorable possible oracle and asks how many SFT
draws are needed to match one optimized draw.  Table~\ref{tab:positioning}
makes these distinct optimization and reporting objects explicit.  This is not
a claim that previous studies report only their best sample: ChipSeek also
reports mean and extreme physical metrics among correct generations.  The
distinction here is retaining the entire draw denominator, including failures,
and testing the resulting endpoint with a frozen paired post-route protocol.
The present controls are internal reward comparisons, not a reproduced
head-to-head evaluation against PPA-RTL or ChipSeek.

\subsection{Physical predictors and reward reliability}

Pre-synthesis performance predictors reduce the cost of exploring FPGA design
spaces~\cite{ustun2020accurate,wu2022highlevel,fang2024presynthesis}.  They are
useful training signals precisely because detailed implementation is costly,
but optimizing a learned reward changes its input distribution.  Reward-model
overoptimization can magnify errors that appear benign on the fitting
distribution~\cite{gao2023scaling}.  We therefore separate the proxy's role
from the reporting authority: the repaired RF and original MLP rewards may
update the policy, whereas only executable correctness and real post-route or
board measurements support physical claims.  The historical trajectory is
used as a bounded diagnostic of this distinction, not as the paper's headline
or as evidence that every physical predictor fails.

\subsection{Scope of the contribution}

The novelty claimed here is not the invention of synthesis-aware reinforcement
learning or of the group-relative policy update.  It is a controlled way to ask
whether training improves the generator itself: the primary object is
the fixed-draw policy distribution, every functional and implementation failure
is retained, real post-route EDA is the reporting authority, and matched arms
compare physical-reward packages with correctness pressure.  The paper does not attempt free-form industrial RTL
optimization, formal equivalence, or universally superior silicon.  Within its
controlled accelerator domain, this scope enables paired design-level analysis,
candidate-level mechanism tests, and fail-closed claim traceability that are not
interchangeable with reporting the best implementation returned by a search.
