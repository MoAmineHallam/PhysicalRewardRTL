\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Language models for RTL generation}

Benchmarks such as VerilogEval~\cite{liu2023verilogeval} and
RTLLM~\cite{lu2024rtllm} established pass@$k$ on small module specifications as
the standard measure, and a series of models has advanced it through
domain-specific pre-training and instruction tuning:
VeriGen~\cite{thakur2024verigen}, RTLCoder~\cite{liu2024rtlcoder},
CodeV~\cite{zhao2024codev} and CraftRTL~\cite{liu2024craftrtl}. Reinforcement
learning has recently been applied to the same objective, with
VeriSeek~\cite{wang2024veriseek} using a verification-derived signal and
VeriReason~\cite{wang2025verireason} combining testbench feedback with
structured reasoning.

What unites this strand is its objective. In every case the reward or metric is
functional---does the module compile, and does it match a reference?---and
physical quality is unmeasured or reported as an afterthought. We adopt the same
starting point, beginning from RTLCoder~\cite{liu2024rtlcoder} and verifying
transfer to Qwen2.5-Coder~\cite{hui2024qwen25coder}, but replace the objective
with a physical one that is gated by, rather than equal to, correctness.

\subsection{Optimising RTL for physical objectives}

A second and very recent strand does target power, performance and area (PPA).
REvolution~\cite{min2025revolution} couples an LLM with an evolutionary search
maintaining separate populations for repair and for PPA improvement;
POET~\cite{poet2026} applies power-oriented evolutionary tuning;
EvolVE~\cite{evolve2026} searches over generation and optimisation;
COEVO~\cite{coevo2026} co-evolves correctness and PPA; Dr.~RTL~\cite{drrtl2026}
performs agentic, tool-grounded self-improvement; and
RTLRewriter~\cite{yao2024rtlrewriter} approaches the problem from the rewriting
side. RTL-OPT~\cite{lu2026rtlopt} contributes a benchmark of $36$ designs paired
with human-optimised references to measure optimisation quality specifically.

Two differences separate these from our work, both consequential. The first is
\emph{where the optimisation lives}. Evolutionary and agentic methods search at
inference time, generating many candidates and selecting or mutating among them,
so the cost of a good design is paid on every invocation. We move the
optimisation into the policy, so a single sample is already the fast design;
Section~\ref{sec:bestofn} quantifies this against an idealised best-of-$N$
selector and shows one sample from our policy exceeding a \emph{perfectly
selected} best of forty-eight supervised samples. The second is that these
methods optimise PPA without a hard correctness precondition on the reward,
which is exactly the configuration in which a physical objective becomes
exploitable; Section~\ref{sec:hacking} documents what that exploitation looks
like in practice.

\subsection{High-level synthesis, PPA prediction, and evaluation practice}

Commercial high-level synthesis (HLS) is the established route from an
algorithmic description to RTL, so we compare against Vitis HLS both without
directives and with hand-placed pipeline, unroll and partition pragmas
(Section~\ref{sec:hls}). Learned models predicting post-synthesis quality from
source-level features have been studied for HLS
designs~\cite{ustun2020accurate,wu2022highlevel}; our frequency surrogate is a
deliberately simple member of that family, and its limitations are central to
Section~\ref{sec:hacking}.

Methodologically, contamination between training and evaluation is a particular
hazard here because parameterised design families make it easy to train on a
$16$-tap filter and evaluate on an $18$-tap one that shares an implementation
template. Reported results in this area are also predominantly synthesis or
timing-analysis estimates rather than device measurements. We depart from
prevailing practice in three ways: a held-out split frozen before training and
enforced programmatically, an explicit separation of interpolation from
extrapolation, and validation on a physical device with a co-resident canary
bounding attribution. We do not claim to be first to place LLM-generated RTL on
an FPGA; we claim the attribution methodology of Section~\ref{sec:silicon} is
more explicit than what is currently reported.
