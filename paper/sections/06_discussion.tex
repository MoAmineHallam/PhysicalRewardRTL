\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the evidence supports}

Correctness-gated policy optimization changes the one-draw distribution of RTL
quality.  The central evidence is the sealed equal-sample endpoint: it preserves
the original probability mass, assigns every failure zero, uses real post-route
measurements, and raises mean Fmax by
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfSftGain}~MHz over
\studyclaim{StudyTwoDesignCount} frozen designs.  Both independent RF seeds are
positive, both regime intervals exclude zero, and a direct secondary comparison
favors RF over the matched MLP reward.  The earlier Qwen-Coder result and
perfect-selector curve broaden the evidence without entering the primary
estimand.

The earlier candidate analysis sharpens a plausible mechanism.  SFT often contains a fast
implementation but assigns it little mass; GRPO concentrates on fewer correct
implementations and makes the fast forms common.  This is a learned generation
policy, not merely a post-hoc reranker, but it is also not evidence of autonomous
microarchitecture invention.  The median exception and mixed recursive-filter
results show that a useful fast form must already be representable and
reward-distinguishable.

The contextual baselines answer narrower objections.  A timing prompt can make
the API model's correct tail fast, but its low correctness makes the one-draw
penalized result weak.  Selected GRPO candidates approach pragma-optimized HLS,
showing that the learned distribution contains competitive implementations;
selection prevents promoting that comparison to the policy-level headline.
VerilogEval reveals the cost of specialization and isolates the claim that GRPO
does not add another general-RTL regression.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

\paragraph{Template-bounded generalization}
The frozen split establishes transfer to unseen parameters and extrapolation
beyond training ranges within the benchmark generators.  It does not test
free-form industrial specifications, control-dominated systems, memories,
multiple clock domains, or cross-template synthesis.

\paragraph{Backbones, runs, and scope}
The second backbone materially reduces a model-specific explanation, but it
uses the earlier \claim{QwenFamilyCount}-family split rather than the complete
primary set.  The sealed result addresses training variance with two RF seeds,
but they share one supervised checkpoint, benchmark generator, and reward-data
construction.  More supervised initializations and reward-model fits would be
needed for a broad variance-components claim.

\paragraph{One target and timing-derived frequency}
All post-route and silicon measurements target one FPGA part.  Candidate
frequency is derived from
worst negative slack after a single implementation flow rather than repeated
timing closure at candidate-specific clocks.  This is symmetric across policies
but is not device-independent ground truth.  The live PYNQ-Z2 sweep supplies an
external physical check, yet its small feasibility-amended subset and shared
bitstream remain insufficient for device-independent generalization.

\paragraph{Secondary-baseline comparability}
The API arms have a smaller sampling budget and represent one prompt and model
configuration.  The HLS comparison changes both input representation and
selection rule.  We therefore use neither as the causal baseline for the GRPO
claim.  They answer whether fast candidates exist under alternative workflows,
not why the policy distribution changed.

\paragraph{Post-hoc uncertainty and mechanism}
The sealed RF-minus-SFT interval plan was frozen before opening the primary
result.  Direct RF-minus-MLP, resource, concentration, and historical trajectory
analyses are post-primary or supporting evidence and are labeled accordingly.
Candidate identity and similar frequency also do not establish semantic
microarchitecture equivalence; the mechanism is a distribution-level
explanation supported by measured support and concentration.

\subsection{Why the reward-hacking result is a subplot}

The trajectory is important operational evidence: early proxy optimization
corresponds to real hardware gain, while later reward growth accompanies flat
conditional frequency and falling correctness.  The lexical source audit makes
the failure concrete, but its canonical rescoring is saturated and lacks
token-identical pairs.  The result therefore motivates periodic real-EDA audits,
proxy-distribution monitoring, and early stopping; it does not establish a
general repair or universal reward-model collapse.

\subsection{Artifact and claim traceability}

The accompanying repository publishes the frozen manifests, sampled RTL with
its original multiplicity, oracle summaries, distinct correct candidates,
post-route PPA rows, canonical analysis, generated tables and figures, and the
human-readable claim ledger.\footnote{Artifact repository:
\url{https://github.com/MoAmineHallam/FPGA}}  Regeneration is fail-closed: the
analysis refuses an incomplete primary design universe, a changed sample
denominator, or a manifest candidate without a physical-design row.  A separate
verifier recomputes every generated result, follows each claim to artifact line
ranges, resolves all included manuscript sources, and rejects empirical numbers
typed directly into authored prose.  This does not eliminate methodological
judgment, but it makes disagreements traceable to a declared computation and
prevents tables from drifting independently of the underlying artifacts.

The repository also distinguishes prepared experiments from completed
measurements.  The new board result enters only because the reserved live-sweep
artifact binds the exact bitstream, HWH, selector map, and amended selection
manifest.  Its failed first selection, range amendment, and one RF loss remain
published.  The same rule keeps exploratory student-model evidence and the
historical asymmetric board comparison outside the quantitative claim set.

\subsection{Evidence intentionally outside the claim set}

Two attractive results remain outside the quantitative manuscript claims.  The
historical silicon sweep uses asymmetric SFT-versus-GRPO selection and is
superseded by the provenance-bound Study~\ref{tab:study2-board} protocol.  The
small-student distillation study has functional training-side evidence but no
complete held-out physical evaluation.  Excluding them is not a judgment that
they are uninteresting; it prevents selected or exploratory observations from
carrying the primary causal conclusion.
