\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the results support}

Our results support a narrower claim than Table~\ref{tab:money} alone suggests,
and we state the narrow version because it is the one that survives scrutiny.
The method \emph{reliably converts a policy that occasionally writes fast
hardware into one that almost always does}, for families in which a fast
implementation style exists within the supervised distribution. It does not
synthesise styles absent from that distribution, and its ceiling is therefore
bounded by the corpus rather than the optimiser. Every result here is consistent
with that statement, including the two families where the gain is small or
absent and the convergence of two base models to identical absolute
frequencies.

The practical corollary for anyone reproducing the method is that effort spent
broadening the \emph{style} diversity of the supervised corpus will likely raise
the ceiling more than effort spent on the reinforcement learning stage, which
principally governs how reliably that ceiling is reached. The comparator-network
family is the clean demonstration: no amount of policy optimisation extracts
frequency from a family whose only reliably-emitted style is combinational.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

\textbf{Domain scope.} We evaluate single-input streaming DSP accelerators with a
uniform interface. This restriction is deliberate and is what makes a per-design
oracle and a uniform hardware harness feasible; the rigour of the evaluation and
the narrowness of the domain are not independent choices. We make no claim about
general RTL generation, and specifically none about control-dominated logic,
memory interfaces or multi-clock designs.

\textbf{Template-generated specifications.} Specifications and supervised
completions are generated programmatically, which guarantees oracle-verifiable
correctness and eliminates contamination from scraped code, but makes the
natural-language inputs more uniform than a practitioner's prose. The
extrapolation regime tests style transfer beyond memorisation; it does not test
robustness to linguistic variation.

\textbf{Single device family.} All synthesis and measurement target one FPGA
(Zynq-7020). Frequency ceilings are device-specific; relative comparisons are
internally consistent because every policy is measured identically, but we do not
claim device independence.

\textbf{Sample sizes.} Held-out evaluation uses $n=48$ per design with two oracle
seeds at length $1024$. The frontier comparison of Section~\ref{sec:frontier}
uses $n=8$ per arm, where one sample shifts a percentage by $12.5$ points, and
should be read as indicative. The surrogate's LODO correlation varies by
approximately $0.07$ between seeds on identical data, so small differences in
that statistic carry little information.

\textbf{Area and latency.} Optimised designs are generally smaller in look-up
tables while consuming more flip-flops, since pipelining trades registers for
frequency; deeper pipelines also increase latency, immaterial for streaming
throughput but not for latency-sensitive use. Multiplier-bearing designs map to
DSP blocks, so LUT counts alone understate resource use, and we report DSP counts
alongside them.

\subsection{On surrogate-guided physical optimisation}

The reward hacking of Section~\ref{sec:hacking} is the most transferable finding
here, because any attempt to optimise a physical objective through a learned
proxy will meet it. Three observations generalise. First, hacking was
\emph{detectable without synthesis}: saturation at the clamp boundary separated
degraded designs from healthy ones, and can be monitored during training at no
cost. Second, the clamp was necessary but not sufficient---it converted an
unbounded exploit (numerical overflow, effectively infinite reward) into a
bounded one, preserving training stability without preventing the policy from
optimising against a fiction where the surrogate was blind. Third, saturation is
a signal to verify, not a verdict: the polynomial family saturated the surrogate
and was genuinely fast, the median family saturated it and was not, and only
measurement distinguishes them. This is why the invariant that surrogate values
never appear in results is load-bearing rather than stylistic---a method
reporting its own reward would have claimed a $4.5\times$ improvement on a
family that improved by nothing.

\subsection{Cost}

The pipeline for one base model comprises approximately three hours of supervised
fine-tuning, seven hours of policy optimisation on a single V100-class device,
and a Vivado pass over the evaluation candidates; distillation into the $1.5$B
student adds twenty-five minutes. The dominant cost is not the reinforcement
learning but the EDA runs required to produce trustworthy labels---itself an
argument for the surrogate, and for re-anchoring it periodically against real
measurements rather than enlarging it.
