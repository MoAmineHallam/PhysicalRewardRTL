\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation of the physical gain}

The sealed study supports a distribution-level improvement within the studied
accelerator generators: the RF-trained policies produce higher timing-derived
physical utility per draw after failures are charged to the originating
policy.  The decomposition locates this gain in the physical quality of
oracle-passing draws, not in higher correctness.  This distinction matters when
using a generator as an engineering tool, because a fast selected example and
a reliably useful draw imply different verification and implementation costs.

The controls constrain, but do not fully identify, the cause.  The original
MLP reward improves on SFT, while RF improves further.  RF changes the predictor
class, canonicalization, and feature representation jointly; their individual
contributions remain unresolved.  Correctness-only training yields more passing
samples but lower physical utility in its single primary seed.  This is a
counterexample to identifying correctness improvement with physical improvement,
not evidence that correctness-only training must fail across seeds or models.

Candidate probabilities suggest a plausible mechanism.  SFT often already
samples a fast implementation, but infrequently; optimization increases mass
on fewer correct outputs.  The sealed support exceptions and finite draw budget
prevent a claim that all optimized implementations were already available in
the observed SFT sample.  The illustrative FIR pair supplies a concrete
direct-to-transposed structural contrast and a changed DSP/LUT mapping, but does
not identify a universal topology change behind the aggregate effect.

\subsection{Implications for CAD workflows}

A learned reward is useful only insofar as it orders enough on-policy
candidates to guide optimization.  The measured reward resolution and sealed
efficacy support that role for the repaired package; they do not validate every
prediction.  The historical late trajectory demonstrates why proxy progress
cannot replace implementation evidence.  Canonicalization reduces sensitivity
to formatting and names, while compilation and sampled trace checks remain
necessary to test the actual reward path.

The physical benefit comes with engineering costs.  RF uses more LUTs and
registers, fewer DSP blocks, and slightly more vectorless power on common
correct support.  Implementations may also differ in latency within the
oracle's alignment window: higher clock frequency is not automatically lower
end-to-end latency.  A deployed design would need explicit resource, latency,
and power constraints, or a multi-objective reward, rather than a frequency-only
preference.  The matched VerilogEval regression further argues for reporting
general competence alongside domain-specific physical gains.

The empirical perfect-selector crossing describes inference workloads on a
finite support.  It omits the cost of domain SFT, reward labels, and policy
training.  Matching non-flat optimizer updates also leaves attempted groups
and compute unequal between arms.  Consequently, the present evidence does not
establish an end-to-end runtime advantage, energy saving, or deployment
break-even point.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

\paragraph{Template-bounded generalization}
The frozen split excludes evaluated designs from domain training, but tests
new parameters within known generators rather than new specification families.
It does not establish competence on control-dominated systems, memories,
multiple clocks, or free-form industrial RTL.  The median family occurs only
under extrapolation, confounding family and regime in that cell.  The separate
Qwen-Coder result broadens backbone evidence but uses the earlier, narrower
family universe.  Neither split construction nor VerilogEval evaluation can
exclude unknown overlap with a base model's pretraining corpus.

\paragraph{Training variance and statistical resolution}
The RF seeds share one SFT checkpoint, reward dataset, and benchmark generator;
they are policy-training replications, not independent full-pipeline repeats.
The correctness-only primary control has one seed.  Design-bootstrap intervals
condition on these realized checkpoints and the frozen task universe.  Small,
unequal family-by-regime cells limit their resolution.  Post-primary
unstratified and leave-one-family-out checks preserve the direction but do not
remove these limitations.  RF versus SFT is the sole prespecified primary
contrast; the other intervals are secondary, without multiplicity adjustment.

\paragraph{Repair and validation history}
The canonical-feature RF hypothesis was motivated by the historical MLP
failure.  Its efficacy test was frozen before the sealed split was opened,
but the hypothesis was not selected blindly.  The earlier all-nonempty-candidate
validation contract also failed on an oracle-rejected completion.  The revised
reward-eligible contract reused unchanged checkpoints and an unseen efficacy
split, but its population was chosen after diagnosing that failure.  These
facts limit how independently the repair and validation scope were selected.

\paragraph{Functional and physical validity}
The oracle checks sampled, latency-aligned traces with a near-unity threshold,
not exact equivalence over all inputs.  Stronger exact-match and formal audits
would be needed to elevate this correctness claim.  The original physical
endpoint comes from one device and a single requested-period flow.  Its script
does not fail closed when a valid setup path is absent: the slack variable has
a zero default.  The retained primary result has not been reclassified on the
basis of that potential failure mode; a complete path-coverage audit is needed
before asserting that every row satisfies a stronger timing contract.

The setup-closure pilot adds constraint-coverage checks and period bracketing
on a small selected subset, under a newer Vivado version and constraints applied
before synthesis.  It supports relative ordering there, but the reverse-FIR
outlier prevents interpreting the median error as universal calibration.
The pilot does not enforce hold or pulse-width closure and therefore does not
establish full timing sign-off.  Cross-tool, cross-device, and operating-corner
generalization remain untested.

\paragraph{Conditional costs, silicon, and baseline comparability}
Resource and power comparisons condition on common correct support, whereas
the primary endpoint penalizes failures.  The board subset was amended for
correctness feasibility and sweep coverage before measurement, uses the same
device family, and includes an RF loss.  It is descriptive support, not an
independent population confirmation.  API prompting and selected-candidate
HLS have different budgets and reporting objects.  The study also lacks an
executed matched DPO or recent external RTL-optimization baseline; internal
reward controls do not establish superiority over those methods.

\subsection{Artifact and claim traceability}

The artifact package contains frozen manifests, sampled RTL and multiplicity,
oracle summaries, implementation rows and failures, board traces,
canonicalization audits, and generated claim ledgers.\footnote{Repository:
\url{https://github.com/MoAmineHallam/FPGA}.  The final release must identify the
exact manuscript and artifact revision.}  Regeneration rejects missing design
coverage, changed denominators, missing implementation rows, stale generated
outputs, and unresolved numerical claims.  The completed post-primary
decomposition, setup-closure pilot, and matched VerilogEval archive are
validated independently before their claims enter the manuscript.

Additional policy-training runs, prepared baseline protocols, and benchmark
preflights are not reported as completed physical evaluations.  The historical
asymmetric board comparison and incomplete smaller-student evaluation remain
excluded.  New seed endpoints must be reported as a separate extension rather
than silently changing the original sealed population or interval.
