\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

This section presents the proposed method in the order in which its components
constrain one another: the correctness oracle that defines admissibility
(Section~\ref{sec:oracle}), the frequency surrogate that makes the physical
objective affordable together with the safeguards that make it survivable
(Section~\ref{sec:surrogate}), the correctness-gated reward and the policy
optimisation procedure built on it (Section~\ref{sec:grpo}), and the supervised
warm start without which the reinforcement learning stage has no foothold
(Section~\ref{sec:sft}). Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the resulting pipeline
and, equally importantly, the boundary between quantities that are used for
training and quantities that are permitted to appear in results.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering

Surrogate values never leave the training loop: every frequency reported in
Section~\ref{sec:results} originates from WNS-derived Vivado synthesis or from

\subsection{Correctness oracle}
\label{sec:oracle}

Let $\mathcal{D}$ denote a design, specified by a natural-language description of
its function together with an exact module interface (port names, directions and
widths). Let $r_{\mathcal{D}}: \mathbb{Z}^{T} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}^{T}$ be the
reference behaviour of $\mathcal{D}$, expressed as an executable integer function
over an input stream of length $T$ with the same finite-precision semantics as
the hardware (all arithmetic modulo $2^{16}$ in our families).

A candidate module $m$ is admitted if and only if it is behaviourally
indistinguishable from the reference on the observable interface. Concretely,
$m$ is compiled with Icarus Verilog, driven by a vector player that applies a
stimulus sequence $x_{1:T}$, and its output stream $y_{1:T}^{m}$ is compared
against $r_{\mathcal{D}}(x_{1:T})$. Because a pipelined implementation and a
combinational one differ in latency but not in function, the comparison is
performed over an alignment offset:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{match}(m) \;=\; \max_{0 \le \ell \le L}
\; \frac{1}{T'} \sum_{t=1}^{T'}
\mathbb{I}\!\left[\, y^{m}_{t+\ell+w} = r_{\mathcal{D}}(x)_{t+w} \,\right],
\label{eq:align}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ ranges over admissible pipeline latencies up to $L=40$ cycles, $w$
discards a warm-up window, and $T'$ is the length of the overlapping region. The
candidate is admitted if $\mathrm{match}(m) = 1$ exactly. Partial credit is
never awarded: a design that matches on $99\%$ of samples is incorrect hardware,
and an earlier version of this work that used trace similarity as a reward
learned to produce plausible-looking but wrong filters. The stimulus combines
directed patterns (impulses, steps, alternating extremes, saturating ramps) with
pseudo-random sequences, and---as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:setup}---the
seeds used at evaluation time differ from those used during training so that a
policy cannot overfit to the specific vectors it was rewarded on.

The oracle is the sole authority on correctness in this work. No syntactic
similarity, embedding distance or compile-only check is used at any stage, for
training or for evaluation.

\subsection{Frequency surrogate and its safeguards}
\label{sec:surrogate}

Obtaining a true \fmax{} requires synthesis and place-and-route, which takes on
the order of minutes per candidate. Policy optimisation consumes thousands of
candidates, so a direct physical reward is infeasible; we therefore learn a
surrogate $\hat{f}_{\theta}(m)$ that predicts $\log$ \fmax{} from the source text
of $m$.

The surrogate is a small multilayer perceptron over twelve interpretable
text-derived features, chosen to reflect what limits the register-to-register
critical path in this design space: the maximum number of multiplications
appearing in a single statement, total multiplication count, the number of
clocked always-blocks and non-blocking assignments, indicators for accumulator
patterns and for chained arithmetic, and---added when the comparator-network
family was introduced---counts of ternary operators, comparisons and blocking
assignments. It is trained on $(m, \text{\fmax})$ pairs obtained from real
Vivado runs and evaluated by leave-one-design-out (LODO) cross-validation, so
that the reported correlation measures generalisation to a design the surrogate
has not seen rather than interpolation within one.

Two safeguards govern its use, and both are consequences of failures we
observed rather than precautions imagined in advance.

\paragraph{Clamping} An unconstrained surrogate is an unbounded optimisation
target. In a pilot run a policy discovered inputs for which the exponentiated
prediction overflowed, obtaining effectively infinite reward for a design whose
true frequency is approximately $191$~MHz. We therefore clamp the predicted
$\log$-frequency to $[\ln 5, \ln 500]$ both at surrogate load time and inside the
reward computation, bounding the exploitable range to physically plausible
values.

\paragraph{Never reporting surrogate values} The clamp bounds the magnitude of an
exploit but does not prevent one. We therefore adopt as an invariant that no
surrogate-derived number is ever reported as a result. The surrogate is a
training-time reward only; every frequency in Section~\ref{sec:results} comes
from a WNS-derived Vivado run or a hardware sweep. Section~\ref{sec:hacking}
shows why this invariant is load-bearing rather than merely fastidious: on one
family the surrogate reports $188.6$~MHz for designs that measure $36$--$40$~MHz.

\subsection{Correctness-gated policy optimisation}
\label{sec:grpo}

Let $\pi_{\phi}$ denote the policy, initialised from the supervised model of
Section~\ref{sec:sft}. For a design $\mathcal{D}$ we sample a group of $G$
candidates $m_1, \dots, m_G \sim \pi_{\phi}(\cdot \mid p_{\mathcal{D}})$ from the
prompt $p_{\mathcal{D}}$. The reward of a candidate is
\begin{equation}
R(m_i) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{clip}\!\big(\hat{f}_{\theta}(m_i),\, 5,\, 500\big)
& \text{if } \mathrm{match}(m_i) = 1,\\[2pt]
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}

Equation~(\ref{eq:reward}) is the central design decision of this work. The two
objectives are composed multiplicatively rather than additively: frequency is
not traded against correctness, it is only accessible \emph{through} correctness.
A degenerate module that emits a constant would close timing at a very high
frequency, but it fails the oracle and receives zero, so no gradient ever pushes
the policy toward it. Conversely, a correct but slow module receives a small
positive reward and is dominated by a correct fast one within the same group.

Rewards are normalised within the group to form advantages,
\begin{equation}
A_i \;=\; \frac{R(m_i) - \mu_R}{\sigma_R + \epsilon},
\qquad
\mu_R = \frac{1}{G}\sum_j R(m_j),
\label{eq:adv}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_R$ the group standard deviation. Group-relative normalisation is
appropriate here because the achievable frequency varies by an order of
magnitude across designs---a six-tap filter and a forty-tap filter are not
comparable in absolute terms---and it removes the need for a learned value
function. When all $G$ rewards coincide the advantage vanishes and the step is
skipped; this occurs on approximately $40\%$ of steps late in training, and is
itself a signal that the policy has converged on those designs.

The policy is updated by ascending
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J}(\phi) \;=\;
\mathbb{E}\Big[ A_i \log \pi_{\phi}(m_i \mid p_{\mathcal{D}}) \Big]
\;-\; \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\!\left[\pi_{\phi} \,\|\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\right],
\label{eq:obj}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a \emph{frozen copy of the supervised policy}, held
as a second, non-trainable adapter loaded alongside the trainable one. This
detail matters: an earlier version anchored the divergence to the un-tuned base
model, which pulls the policy away from the very competence the supervised stage
established. The log-probability in Equation~(\ref{eq:obj}) is computed over the
extracted module only, truncated at the first \texttt{endmodule} token, so that
text generated after the module boundary---which the reward does not
see---contributes no gradient.

Two practical constraints are worth recording because violating them silently
degrades results. Generation must be allocated enough tokens for the largest
design in the family: a transposed $32$-tap filter requires over $750$ tokens,
and a budget of $768$ truncates it mid-module, causing extraction to fail, the
reward to be zero, and the policy to learn to \emph{avoid} the fast form. We use
$1536$ tokens throughout, raised to $3072$ for the largest evaluation design.
Second, the policy is optimised over all $145$ training-split designs rather
than a small subset, which both broadens coverage and reduces per-prompt
overfitting.

\subsection{Supervised warm start}
\label{sec:sft}

Reinforcement learning refines competence; it cannot create it. A cross-model
probe found that un-tuned base models produce correct accelerators of the kind
studied here in roughly $2$--$7\%$ of samples, which leaves the correctness gate
of Equation~(\ref{eq:reward}) closed almost always and the policy with no signal.
We therefore begin with a supervised stage.

The corpus is constructed programmatically rather than scraped. For each design
in the training split we emit the same functional specification in multiple
\emph{implementation styles}---for the multiply--accumulate families a direct
array-and-loop reference, a pipelined form, an unrolled named-register form and a
transposed form; for the polynomial family a combinational Horner chain, a
pipelined variant and an inlined nested form; and analogous alternatives for the
recursive and comparator-network families. Every candidate pair is passed
through the oracle of Section~\ref{sec:oracle} before entering the corpus, so no
incorrect training example exists by construction. This yields $457$ distinct
verified pairs.

Style multiplicity is deliberate and is what later makes the reinforcement
learning stage possible: it establishes a distribution over implementations of
differing speed for the same specification, and the measured spread within that
distribution---a median of $155$~MHz between the slowest and fastest correct
implementation of the same design---is precisely the headroom the physical
reward exploits. Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} shows that the optimiser operates
within this headroom rather than beyond it, which explains both where the method
succeeds and where it does not.
