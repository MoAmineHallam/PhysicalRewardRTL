\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Accelerator domain and sealed split}

The benchmark contains streaming finite-impulse-response (FIR), reverse-FIR,
polynomial, recursive-filter, and median-filter accelerators under a uniform
clocked interface.  The families cover feed-forward arithmetic pipelines,
recurrences, and comparator networks.  Each specification and its executable
reference are generated programmatically, and several implementation styles
provide physically distinct but behaviorally equivalent training examples.
The domain is deliberately controlled: it spans multiple accelerator
structures but not arbitrary industrial RTL.

The sealed evaluation contains \studyclaim{StudyTwoDesignCount} designs,
balanced between \studyclaim{StudyTwoInterpDesignCount} interpolation and
\studyclaim{StudyTwoExtrapDesignCount} extrapolation tasks.  Family quotas are
unequal because the eligible median interpolation pool was empty; the median
family is represented only under extrapolation, so median-family and regime
effects cannot be separated.  The family breakdown in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results} reports the exact composition.  Interpolation changes parameters within
training ranges; extrapolation uses larger or newly parameterized instances
outside those ranges.  Every sealed design is excluded from the SFT corpus,
reward rows, policy prompts, and prior analysis.  The split remained unopened
until all declared checkpoints and pre-open validation artifacts were frozen.

\subsection{Arms, seeds, and sampling}

The primary comparison uses RTLCoder, its completion-masked SFT adapter, and
two independently trained repaired-RF policy adapters.  The MLP arm trains two
matched policies from the same SFT adapter using the original reward.  The
correctness-only arm uses one preregistered full-budget seed.  Arms are matched
on non-flat optimizer updates rather than attempted groups, because a flat
group produces no policy-ranking information.  A crashed run is discarded and
restarted from the frozen SFT state; partial histories are never merged.
Equal non-flat updates do not imply equal training compute: flat groups still
require generation and oracle execution, particularly for correctness-only
rewards.  The primary comparison matches deployment draws, not end-to-end
training time or energy.

At the sealed endpoint, SFT and every trained arm receive
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSamplesPerArmDesign} draws per design at temperature
\studyclaim{StudyTwoEvaluationTemperature}.  Two-seed arms contribute
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSamplesPerSeedDesign} draws per seed.  The midpoint
diagnostic uses \studyclaim{StudyTwoMidSamplesPerSeedDesign} draws per RF seed
and design.  Generation seeds are distinct from training seeds and from the
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOracleStreams} oracle streams, each of which contains
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOracleVectors} test vectors.  Accepted candidates are
deduplicated only for implementation; multiplicity is preserved for every
policy statistic.

\subsection{Post-route implementation and statistical analysis}

The frozen physical flow is \studyclaim{StudyTwoVivadoEnvironment}.  Every
distinct correct candidate is synthesized, placed, and routed with the same
\claim{TargetPeriod}-ns requested clock.  Achievable frequency is derived from
the request and reported worst negative slack.  We therefore use
``post-route timing-derived \fmax'' rather than ``timing closure'': the flow
does not repeatedly rerun implementation at candidate-specific clock periods.
The historical script introduces the clock constraint after synthesis; this
flow is held fixed rather than retroactively corrected in the sealed result.

The primary estimand is Eq.~\eqref{eq:equal-sample}.  Correctness and
conditional frequency are diagnostics, not replacement endpoints.  The
primary RF-minus-SFT confidence plan is frozen and stratified by family and
regime, with percentile bounds.  RF-minus-MLP intervals reuse its exact
resample plans as a bounded post-primary comparison.  The family cells are
small and unequal, so the manuscript adds an unstratified paired-bootstrap and
leave-one-family-out sensitivity but does not replace the frozen primary plan.
No multiplicity adjustment is applied: the RF-versus-SFT contrast is the sole
prespecified primary inference, while control, family, PPA, and mechanism
analyses are secondary or descriptive.  Resource and power analyses condition
on designs with compiled correct support in SFT, MLP, and RF; assigning zero
area or power to a failed sample would incorrectly make failure look efficient.
Power is Vivado's vectorless estimate, not measured board power.

The board study uses within-design SFT/RF pairs and applies the same
multiplicity-aware candidate rule to both policies.  All selected DUTs, a
co-resident echo canary, and the selector interface occupy one bitstream/HWH
pair.  Each complete run sweeps the same clock grid, recomputes the passing
boundary from raw scores, and records the bitstream, HWH, selector-map, and
selection-manifest hashes.

The first outcome-independent family hash selected a reverse-FIR design with no
correct candidate in either paired arm, so that attempt stopped without a
silicon outcome.  Before any board measurement, eligibility was amended to
require a correct candidate in both arms and the same outcome-independent hash
was reapplied.  The common sweep floor was also lowered after sealed Vivado
rows showed that the original range would not bracket every selected DUT.  No
reward, timing, area, power, or resource outcome was used to choose the
replacement.  Nevertheless, these feasibility amendments make the resulting
board subset descriptive rather than independently confirmatory.

\subsection{Supporting and contextual evaluations}

An earlier \claim{DesignCount}-design RTLCoder evaluation provides broader
five-family evidence and the candidate-distribution analysis.  A separate
Qwen-Coder experiment~\cite{hui2024qwen25coder} repeats the full
SFT-to-policy-optimization recipe on
\claim{QwenDesignCount} designs from the earlier
\claim{QwenFamilyCount}-family universe; it is not pooled with the sealed
study.  The post-primary perfect-selector analysis uses the sealed SFT support
and retains the earlier evaluation only as historical context.

Three contextual controls answer narrower questions.  A stored API experiment
uses the same tasks and oracle with \claim{FrontierSamplesPerDesign} samples
per prompt arm.  The HLS comparison measures post-implementation throughput
for no-pragma and engineer-pragma C++ but compares them with the fastest
measured optimized RTL candidate, not a policy average.  The matched post-primary
VerilogEval extension evaluates the sealed SFT and RF checkpoints using
\revisionclaim{VerilogSamples} samples per checkpoint on
\revisionclaim{VerilogProblems} problems.  The earlier base/SFT/policy run uses
a different generation budget and is not pooled with this comparison.
Different budgets and selection rules
prevent the API and HLS results from serving as causal primary baselines.

A completed post-primary pilot reimplements
\revisionclaim{ClosureCandidateCount} selected SFT/RF candidates with
candidate-specific period bracketing under Vivado
\revisionclaim{ClosureVersion}.  Constraints precede synthesis in that flow.
It therefore tests sensitivity to both constraint handling and tool version,
not the timing estimator alone.  Its pass criterion verifies setup slack,
constraint coverage, and routing, but does not enforce hold or pulse-width
closure.  We report it as a descriptive setup-closure sensitivity and do not
replace the primary population endpoint with this selected subset.

\subsection{Study status and reproducibility}

An earlier validation attempt used a stricter contract over every nonempty RF
training completion.  It stopped before sealed efficacy when that contract
encountered a malformed, oracle-rejected completion.  After diagnosing the
scope mismatch, the present study froze a reward-eligible contract over exactly
the candidates that could influence the RF reward, reused the already-trained
checkpoints without retraining or selection, and opened a still-unseen sealed
efficacy split only after the revised gate passed.  The efficacy test is
prospective with respect to that split, but it is not an independent validation
of the post-diagnosis gate scope.

All frozen inputs and outputs are content-hashed.  Generated claim ledgers bind
manuscript values to artifact line ranges; figure manifests additionally store
the exact plotted arrays and input/output hashes.  Failed protocol attempts,
implementation failures, board amendments, and excluded exploratory results
remain in the public record rather than being overwritten.
