\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Design families}

We study five families of single-input streaming DSP accelerators, chosen to
span three structurally distinct circuit classes rather than to maximise their
number. All share a common interface (a clock, an active-low reset, an $8$-bit
input sample and a $16$-bit registered output), which is what makes a uniform
per-design oracle and a uniform hardware harness possible.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Multiply--accumulate datapaths.} \texttt{fir} ($T$-tap finite
impulse response filters with fixed coefficients, $T \in [4,32]$) and
\texttt{firr} (the same structure with a different coefficient family). The
critical path is a chain of multiplications and additions, and the fast
implementation is the transposed, pipelined form.

\item \textbf{Polynomial evaluation.} \texttt{poly} (degree-$D$ polynomial
evaluation modulo $2^{16}$, $D \in [2,10]$, with six coefficient variants per
degree). The natural implementation is a Horner recurrence, which is
sequentially dependent and therefore hard for automatic pipelining to break.

\item \textbf{Recursive filters.} \texttt{iir} (order-$N$ infinite impulse
response filters, $N \in [2,14]$, four coefficient variants each). Unlike the
preceding families these contain genuine feedback, so the loop-carried
dependency bounds the achievable frequency independently of pipelining depth.

\item \textbf{Comparator networks.} \texttt{med} (running median over a window of
width $W$). This family contains no multipliers at all; its cost is a sorting
network of comparisons, making it structurally unlike the other four.
\end{itemize}

A sixth family (\texttt{cordic}) was constructed and is retained in the
supervised corpus but excluded from reinforcement learning; the reason is
reported as a negative result in Section~\ref{sec:negative}.

\subsection{Frozen held-out protocol}
\label{sec:holdout}

The held-out split was fixed before any model reported in this paper was
trained, and is enforced by a single predicate \texttt{is\_holdout()} that is
imported by the corpus generator, the reinforcement learning design list and the
surrogate's data loader. Each consumer refuses to proceed if a held-out design
appears; during this work the surrogate loader in fact rejected $345$ labelled
rows that would otherwise have leaked, which we regard as evidence that the
guard is doing real work rather than decorating the method.

The split distinguishes two regimes:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Interpolation} --- parameters inside the trained range, withheld:
\texttt{fir} and \texttt{firr} with $T \in \{6,10,18,26\}$; all \texttt{poly}
variants of degree $7$; \texttt{iir} of orders $\{5,9\}$; \texttt{med} with
$W=7$. This regime asks whether the policy generalises across the parameter
grid.

\item \textbf{Extrapolation} --- parameters outside the trained range, so these
designs are never produced by the training grid at all and are constructed
directly at evaluation time: \texttt{fir} and \texttt{firr} with
$T \in \{36,40\}$; two new \texttt{poly} coefficient variants of degrees
$\{4,8\}$; \texttt{iir} of orders $\{16,20\}$; \texttt{med} with $W=11$. This
regime asks whether the implementation \emph{style} transfers beyond the sizes
seen in training.
\end{itemize}

This yields $145$ training designs and $30$ held-out designs ($19$ interpolation,
$11$ extrapolation). Evaluation prompts for held-out designs are constructed
directly from the specification and interface rather than drawn from the corpus
generator; the interface header is part of the problem statement, not leakage.

\subsection{Models and training configuration}

The primary base model is RTLCoder-6.7B~\cite{liu2024rtlcoder}; transfer is
verified on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B~\cite{hui2024qwen25coder} and capability
compression on Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B. All stages use low-rank adaptation
(LoRA, $r=16$) so that the specialisation is detachable, which
Section~\ref{sec:regression} shows to be a meaningful property.

Supervised fine-tuning runs for four epochs over the $457$-pair corpus with the
loss masked to completion tokens. Policy optimisation runs for $400$ steps with
group size $G=8$, temperature $1.0$, learning rate $10^{-5}$, KL coefficient
$\beta = 0.1$ against the frozen supervised adapter, and a generation budget of
$1536$ tokens. Training uses oracle seed $0$ with stimulus length $512$.

\subsection{Measurement}

\paragraph{Synthesis} All reported frequencies are obtained from Vivado 2023.1
targeting a Xilinx Zynq-7020 (\texttt{xc7z020clg400-1}) with a $5$~ns target
period, taking \fmax{} from the post-implementation worst negative slack. A
design that fails to close or fails synthesis is recorded as such rather than
dropped.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol} Each policy is sampled $n=48$ times per held-out
design. Correctness at evaluation time is assessed with oracle seeds $1$ and $2$
and stimulus length $1024$---deliberately different from the training seed and
four times longer---so that a policy that had overfitted to the exact training
vectors would be exposed. Distinct implementations are deduplicated by
normalised source, and each surviving implementation carries the number of
samples that collapsed onto it. Per-design frequency is the frequency-weighted
mean over distinct correct implementations, which is the expected quality of
\emph{one} sample from that policy; incorrect samples contribute zero. This is a
deliberately conservative statistic: it charges a policy for its failures rather
than reporting only its best output.

\paragraph{Baselines} We report the un-tuned base model, the supervised model,
and a \emph{best-of-eight} baseline in which the surrogate selects the best of
the first eight supervised samples. The last of these is the fair
inference-cost comparison: it asks whether the gains of the proposed method could
be obtained more cheaply by sampling the supervised model repeatedly and
choosing well. Section~\ref{sec:bestofn} strengthens this baseline further by
computing the expected best-of-$N$ under a \emph{perfect} selector in closed
form, which upper-bounds any reranking scheme.

\paragraph{Silicon} Held-out design pairs are placed into a single bitstream on a
PYNQ-Z2 board together with a canary module of independently known frequency,
and the fabric clock is swept from $30$~MHz upward in $5$~MHz steps, three runs
per configuration, with on-chip comparison against a golden output stream. A
design's measured \fmax{} is the highest frequency at which it matches the
golden stream on every run. Placing the compared designs in the same bitstream
ensures identical placement, routing and thermal conditions; the canary bounds
what may be attributed to the design under test rather than to the harness.
