\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Correctness-gated policy optimization raises equal-sample post-route frequency
from \claim{OverallSftPenalized} to \claim{OverallGrpoPenalized}~MHz across
\claim{DesignCount} frozen held-out designs.  The gain occurs under both
interpolation and extrapolation, appears on \claim{OverallImproved} individual
designs, and survives assigning zero frequency to every incorrect or failed
sample.  A second backbone independently improves all
\claim{QwenDesignCount} designs in its narrower replication.

Candidate-level evidence supports a distribution-shift account: optimization
increases mass on a smaller set of fast correct implementations that supervised
sampling often already contains.  The perfect-selector analysis quantifies the
resulting sampling efficiency, while the API, HLS, and VerilogEval controls
separate one-draw utility, selected implementation quality, and general-RTL
regression.

The late reward trajectory supplies the necessary warning.  Proxy reward can
continue rising after measured conditional frequency has flattened and
equal-sample performance has fallen.  The strongest conclusion is therefore
neither open-ended circuit invention nor trust in the proxy: correctness-gated
optimization can make fast accelerator RTL substantially more likely, provided
that executable oracles and real EDA artifacts remain the final authority.
