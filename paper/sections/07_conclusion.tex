\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper has argued that functional correctness is an insufficient objective
for machine-generated register-transfer level design, and has proposed a
correctness-gated reinforcement learning method that optimises a physical
quantity---maximum operating frequency---while treating correctness as a
precondition rather than a competing term. The reward of a generated module is
its surrogate-predicted frequency if an input/output-equivalence oracle certifies
it correct, and exactly zero otherwise, so that frequency can never be purchased
with correctness.

Evaluated on thirty held-out DSP accelerator designs spanning five families and
three circuit classes, under a split frozen before training and enforced
programmatically, the method raises mean WNS-derived \fmax{} from $86.5$ to
$211.3$~MHz under parameter interpolation and from $78.5$ to $174.2$~MHz under
extrapolation, exceeding a perfect-selector best-of-eight baseline in both
regimes. A single sample from the optimised policy exceeds the expected best of
forty-eight perfectly selected supervised samples in the interpolation regime,
and on two designs the supervised model never produces the fast implementation at
all---an existence failure that no selection procedure can repair. Five held-out
design pairs measured on a Zynq-7020 sustain $2.25$--$3.40\times$ the clock of
the supervised baseline, with zero spread across repeated sweeps and with a
co-resident canary bounding attribution. The recipe transfers unmodified to a
second base model, and both models converge to the same measured frequency
ceiling, indicating that the optimisation locates a property of the design space
rather than of the generator.

Per-candidate analysis identifies the mechanism precisely: the method reallocates
probability mass onto the fastest implementation style the policy already
possesses, rather than discovering faster hardware. This claim is falsifiable and
predicts its own limits, correctly identifying the recursive-filter family (where
the supervised distribution already emitted fast forms frequently, leaving little
to reallocate) and the comparator-network family (where the style that made the
family correct is combinational by construction, so no fast alternative exists to
amplify) as the cases in which the method yields little or no frequency gain.

We further document a caught case of reward hacking: the learned surrogate,
whose leave-one-design-out correlation falls from $0.965$ to $0.75$--$0.82$ once
structurally dissimilar circuit classes are added, reported $188.6$~MHz for
median filters measuring $36$--$40$~MHz in silicon-bound synthesis, while
saturating identically on a polynomial family that genuinely reached
$191$--$193$~MHz. Saturation therefore indicates that the reward has stopped
carrying information and warrants verification, but---contrary to the more
attractive hypothesis, which we tested and could not confirm---it does not by
itself predict correctness degradation. This is the concrete justification for
the invariant maintained throughout this work, that no surrogate-derived number
is ever reported as a result.

Finally, the learned capability compresses into a $1.5$~billion-parameter student
for four of five families in twenty-five minutes of training, using a
distillation corpus every row of which is oracle-verified; the fifth family fails
entirely, marking a capability--size frontier that mirrors the capability-class
ceiling observed for the CORDIC family at $7$~billion parameters on two
independent base models.

Three directions follow directly from the limits identified here. The first is to
broaden implementation-style diversity in the supervised corpus, since the
analysis indicates that the ceiling is set there rather than by the optimiser;
the pipelined median template that would repair the comparator-network result is
the immediate instance. The second is to replace text-derived surrogate features
with structural features extracted from an early synthesis pass, which is the
principled remedy for the cross-circuit-class degradation reported in
Section~\ref{sec:hacking} and a prerequisite for extending the method beyond
datapath-dominated designs. The third is to apply the correctness-gated
formulation to design families with different admissibility contracts---latency
insensitive or transaction-equivalent rather than cycle-comparable---which would
test whether the structure of the method, rather than its present instantiation,
is what generalises.
