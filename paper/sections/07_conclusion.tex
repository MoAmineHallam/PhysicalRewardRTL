\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Correctness-gated policy optimization with the RF physical reward raises
equal-sample post-route frequency from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallSftPenalized} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfPenalized}~MHz across
\studyclaim{StudyTwoDesignCount} frozen held-out designs.  Both independent
training seeds improve, the paired intervals exclude zero under interpolation
and extrapolation, and the gain survives assigning zero frequency to every
incorrect or failed sample.  RF also exceeds the matched MLP reward, while the
correctness-only control does not reproduce the physical gain.

The improvement carries a defensible physical trade-off: more LUTs and
flip-flops, fewer DSP blocks, and a small vectorless power increase on common
support.  A live PYNQ-Z2 sweep supplies heterogeneous descriptive support with
two RF wins, two ties, one loss, and exactly repeatable Fmax boundaries; its
feasibility amendments prevent treating that small subset as independent
confirmation.

Earlier candidate-level evidence supports a distribution-shift account: optimization
increases mass on a smaller set of fast correct implementations that supervised
sampling often already contains.  The perfect-selector analysis quantifies the
resulting sampling efficiency, while the API, HLS, and VerilogEval controls
separate one-draw utility, selected implementation quality, and general-RTL
regression.

The bounded late reward trajectory supplies the necessary warning.  Proxy reward can
continue rising after measured conditional frequency has flattened and
equal-sample performance has fallen.  The strongest conclusion is therefore
neither open-ended circuit invention nor trust in the proxy: correctness-gated
optimization can make fast accelerator RTL substantially more likely, provided
that executable oracles and real EDA artifacts remain the final authority.
