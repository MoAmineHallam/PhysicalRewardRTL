\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Correctness-gated policy optimization raises the equal-sample, post-route
frequency of generated accelerator RTL from \claim{OverallSftPenalized} to
\claim{OverallGrpoPenalized}~MHz across \claim{DesignCount} frozen held-out
designs.  The gain appears on \claim{OverallImproved} designs, remains positive
under interpolation and extrapolation bootstrap analyses, and is measured after
assigning zero frequency to every incorrect or failed sample.  One optimized
draw also matches many draws from the supervised policy even when the latter is
given an unattainable perfect selector.

The evidence supports a distribution-shift claim: optimization makes already
learned fast implementation styles more likely.  It does not support claims of
open-ended circuit invention, preserved correctness in every regime, or
universal reward-model collapse.  The bounded trajectory case shows why this
distinction matters: proxy reward can continue rising after real conditional
frequency has flattened, so real EDA artifacts must remain the reporting
authority.  A symmetric silicon sweep and independent policy-training seeds are
the clearest next validations.
