\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Executable RTL benchmarks ask whether generated code compiles and matches a
reference behavior.  Physical design adds a separate capability: two
cycle-compatible implementations can compute the same function yet have very
different critical paths.  A generator trained only for correctness has no
reason to prefer the form that closes timing at a useful clock, while selecting
many candidates after generation shifts the cost to inference and EDA.

Training against physical quality is difficult for the opposite reason.
Post-route implementation is too expensive to place inside every policy update,
so a learned proxy is attractive.  Once optimized, however, that proxy is least
trustworthy exactly where the policy concentrates probability.  Reporting the
training reward as achieved frequency would confuse the objective with the
evidence.  We therefore impose two boundaries: functional equivalence gates the
reward, and real post-route implementation gates every physical claim in the
paper.  Incorrect samples, missing candidates, and implementation failures all
receive zero frequency in the primary endpoint.

The strongest experiment is a separately frozen
\studyclaim{StudyTwoDesignCount}-design evaluation with matched sample cost and
two independent optimized training seeds.  Penalized equal-sample frequency
rises from \studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallSftPenalized} to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfPenalized}~MHz.  The paired RF-reward gain is
\studyclaim{StudyTwoInterpRfSftGain}~MHz under interpolation and
\studyclaim{StudyTwoExtrapRfSftGain}~MHz under extrapolation; both frozen
intervals exclude zero.  The two seed-specific overall effects are
\studyclaim{StudyTwoRfSeedOneOverallGain} and
\studyclaim{StudyTwoRfSeedTwoOverallGain}~MHz.  Correctness changes only from
\studyclaim{StudyTwoSftCorrectness}\% to
\studyclaim{StudyTwoRfCorrectness}\%, so the physical gain is not purchased by a
collapse in functional validity.

Matched controls sharpen the claim.  The random-forest reward exceeds an
otherwise matched multilayer-perceptron reward by
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpGain}~MHz, with a paired secondary interval of
[\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpCiLow},
\studyclaim{StudyTwoOverallRfMlpCiHigh}]~MHz.  A correctness-only control does
not reproduce the physical gain.  An earlier, separate evaluation and a
Qwen-Coder replication~\cite{hui2024qwen25coder} remain supporting evidence;
they are not pooled with the sealed study.

The evaluation is organized around four questions.  \emph{Capability} asks
whether optimization improves the performance of one draw when sampling cost
is held fixed and all failures remain in the denominator.  \emph{Transfer}
asks whether the direction survives a second backbone and extrapolated design
parameters.  \emph{Mechanism} asks whether the policy discovers wholly new
support or reallocates probability among implementations already reachable
after supervision.  \emph{Reliability} asks when the learned reward ceases to
track real physical design.  This separation prevents a selected fast example,
a proxy score, or a conditional-on-correct statistic from substituting for the
policy-level result.

This paper makes four contributions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item a correctness-gated policy objective that combines an executable
    input/output-equivalence oracle with a learned physical reward while
    preserving a frozen supervised-policy KL anchor;
    \item a separately frozen interpolation/extrapolation study with two
    independent RF-reward seeds, matched MLP-reward and correctness-only
    controls, and a failure-penalized post-route primary endpoint;
    \item conditional resource and vectorless-power trade-offs plus a live
    PYNQ-Z2 clock-sweep study whose protocol amendments and heterogeneous
    outcomes remain explicit;
    \item an earlier second-backbone replication, perfect-selector comparison,
    and contextual HLS, API, and general-RTL controls kept outside the primary
    estimand; and
    \item a candidate-distribution explanation plus a bounded reward-
    overoptimization subplot, all generated from artifact-backed claim ledgers.
\end{itemize}

The supported mechanism is probability reallocation, not open-ended circuit
invention.  Supervision already exposes several implementations; optimization
places more probability on fast ones.  That distinction explains both the
large FIR, reverse-FIR, and polynomial gains and the weaker recursive and
median cases.  It also motivates the paper's central methodological rule: the
proxy may train the policy, but only functional simulation and real EDA may
evaluate it.
