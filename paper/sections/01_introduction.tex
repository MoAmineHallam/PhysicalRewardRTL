\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Code-generation benchmarks usually ask whether a generated RTL module compiles
and matches a reference behavior.  Hardware implementation adds another axis:
two cycle-compatible modules can be functionally indistinguishable yet have
very different critical paths.  A policy rewarded only for correctness has no
reason to prefer the implementation that meets a demanding clock.

Optimizing physical quality with a language model creates a second problem.
Post-route implementation is too expensive to place inside every policy update,
so a learned proxy is attractive; once that proxy becomes the objective,
however, the policy searches precisely where its errors matter most.  Reporting
the proxy would then conflate reward with evidence.  Our design therefore draws
a hard boundary: a proxy guides training, while every paper result is recomputed
from real post-route artifacts.  A functional failure, a missing implementation,
or a failed implementation receives zero frequency in the primary endpoint.

We study whether correctness-gated policy optimization changes the probability
of emitting fast RTL, rather than merely finding an occasional fast candidate.
The evaluation split was frozen before training and contains
\claim{DesignCount} parameterized designs across \claim{FamilyCount} families.
Each policy contributes the same \claim{SamplesPerDesign} original samples per
design.  Under interpolation, the primary endpoint moves from
\claim{InterpSftPenalized} to \claim{InterpGrpoPenalized}~MHz; under
extrapolation it moves from \claim{ExtrapSftPenalized} to
\claim{ExtrapGrpoPenalized}~MHz.  The corresponding paired gains have bootstrap
intervals [\claim{InterpCiLow}, \claim{InterpCiHigh}] and
[\claim{ExtrapCiLow}, \claim{ExtrapCiHigh}]~MHz.  Improvement occurs on
\claim{OverallImproved} of \claim{DesignCount} designs rather than being driven
by one outlier.

This paper makes the following contributions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item a correctness-gated training objective that makes functional
    equivalence a prerequisite for physical reward;
    \item a frozen interpolation/extrapolation protocol whose primary metric is
    equal-sample post-route frequency, with failures scored as zero;
    \item a machine-generated claim ledger connecting every reported number to
    artifact line ranges, so prose and tables cannot drift from analysis; and
    \item a bounded reward-overoptimization case study that compares proxy and
    measured trajectories without turning an observed surrogate failure into a
    general claim about learned rewards.
\end{itemize}

The main result is deliberately narrower than ``reinforcement learning invents
better circuits.''  Candidate-level inspection indicates that supervision
already exposes several implementation styles and policy optimization
reallocates probability toward the faster ones.  Families without a fast,
reliably generated style show correspondingly smaller gains.  The method
therefore improves sampling efficiency inside a template-bounded design space;
it does not establish open-ended circuit invention.
