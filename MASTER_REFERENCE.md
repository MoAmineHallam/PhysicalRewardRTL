## 0. Current plan: physical-quality scaling of LLM-generated hardware

Proposed 2026-10-03; the detailed version is the
[plan](research/fpga2/PHYSICAL_SCALING_PLAN_20261003.md). Sections 1–10 record the
earlier architecture direction and its evidence; they remain the record of that
work.

### The idea

Academic groups, EDA vendors and chip startups now train LLMs to write RTL, some
with RL on PPA rewards. Scaling laws tell practitioners how to spend on model size,
data and compute for loss or pass rate, but nobody has measured how the **physical
quality of the generated hardware** scales. This study measures it with the
submitted paper's endpoint, `F = q·μ` (correctness × multiplicity-weighted
post-route frequency of correct circuits, failures scored zero). Five axes are
varied in a controlled way within the Qwen2.5-Coder family:

1. model size (0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B; 14B if needed);
2. supervised data, both quantity and implementation-style coverage;
3. RL compute, compared with best-of-k selection at matched cost;
4. feedback fidelity: learned proxy → Yosys → Vivado post-synthesis → Vivado
   post-route → ASIC post-synthesis and post-route (OpenROAD) → PYNQ-Z2 silicon,
   with a transfer matrix;
5. compression (FP16, GPTQ-4, GPTQ-3).

**Deliverables:**
- fitted relationships, or ceilings, for `q` and `μ` separately;
- iso-cost guidance: a bigger model, more RL, or more samples;
- whether cheap FPGA feedback trains gains that hold on ASIC and silicon;
- a released dataset of all generated circuits with their implementation reports.

**Relation to the supervisor's goal:** this is a contribution to how chips are
designed by AI, not a new neural block. The FFN screens, Gate 0 and the literature
audits indicate that a new-block paper is unlikely to be defensible at a CCF-A
venue with four V100s. The results speak directly to the budget decisions of any
chip or AI-EDA team.

### Why the evidence points here

- **Compression is likely flat at W4.** Gate 0: GPTQ-4 leaves generated-circuit
  frequency unchanged, so the compression axis is cheap and short.
- **The SFT data may cap `μ`.** RL concentrates on circuits the SFT policy already
  emits: under GPTQ-4 the RL policy stays on its majority circuit for 83% of
  correct draws, with support 0.71 against 2.04 for SFT. Hypothesis: `μ` is capped
  by the styles in the SFT data rather than by model size.
- **Size has signal at least in `q`.** The 1.5B student fails the median family
  while the 7B does not.

## 1. Objective and the agreed two-stage sequence

The supervisor-directed first question is:

> Can we design a transformer block whose mathematical structure reduces data movement and simplifies the accelerator, while preserving model quality?

The intended outcome is a model–accelerator pair suitable for a more efficient digital chip. FPGA implementation is a development and validation platform. A claim about an ASIC additionally requires an explicit technology library, memory implementation, and physical-design flow.

The project has two stages:

1. **Architecture and hardware:** define and train candidate transformer blocks, establish their quality, and implement their computation and memory schedules. Determine whether changing the mathematics improves a measured quality–hardware trade-off beyond strong existing alternatives.
2. **Knowledge and task adaptation:** distill an existing LLM into the selected student architecture, then apply supervised domain adaptation and the physical-reward policy-training methodology developed in the submitted RTL paper. Recheck both model execution cost and generated RTL quality after adaptation.

Stage 1 includes ordinary language-model training: an untrained block cannot demonstrate preserved model quality. Large-teacher distillation and physical-reward policy fine-tuning are reserved for Stage 2. Pilot training is a feasibility screen, not a demonstration of competitive large-model capability.

The working interpretation of the last step is a small, hardware-efficient **RTL-generating student**, following the submitted paper. This interpretation follows the requested reuse of that paper's method. The Stage 1 architecture question is independent of the downstream RTL application.

### Two distinct physical outcomes

| Outcome | What determines it | How it is evaluated |
|---|---|---|
| Cost of executing the student | Its block structure, precision, mapping, memory hierarchy, and workload | Prefill/decode latency, bytes transferred, area/resources, and appropriately measured or estimated energy |
| Quality of circuits generated by the student | Its learned output distribution and correctness | Functional verification and independent physical implementation, retaining all sampled failures |

Improving generated RTL does **not** by itself reduce the cost of executing a fixed-shape student. An inference-cost reward constant across outputs has no useful within-group ranking signal. Architecture search or an explicit variable execution policy would be needed to directly optimize that cost. Do not conflate these two outcomes in a paper or reward equation.

## 2. Research assessment

**Recommendation:** begin with the feed-forward sublayer and its channel communication inside an otherwise fixed causal transformer block. Study a small, explicit family of structured transformations and their realizable memory schedules. Keep attention, normalization, tokenizer, and numerical precision matched initially.

This is a tractable entry point rather than a claim that FFNs always dominate. Profile first: short-context, small-batch decoding, long-context decoding, and prefill can have different bottlenecks. If the selected workload is dominated by the KV cache, an FFN-only intervention may have insufficient end-to-end value.

**Novelty is unresolved.** The literature already answers the broad question positively in several settings. A publishable contribution must identify a specific improvement over those settings or methods. The current search supports a focused experimental hypothesis, not a claim that no one has studied communication-aware transformers.

The full [literature ledger](research/fpga2/RELATED_WORK.md) records what each paper did, its evidence scope, and its implications. The [search record](research/fpga2/SEARCH_RECORD.md) records coverage and remaining checks.

### Ideas that are already established

| Broad proposal | Prior work that prevents a broad novelty claim |
|---|---|
| Use structured matrices instead of dense projections | Butterfly factorization, Monarch, FTRANS, FABNet |
| Use grouped feed-forward transformations | GroupBERT |
| Share low-rank bases or input projections | Basis Sharing, SkipCat, LatentLLM |
| Avoid materializing attention intermediates | FlashAttention, FLAT |
| Fuse a larger part of the transformer computation | TransFusion |
| Search architectures with a hardware cost | HAT, TransCODE, Quasar-ViT, LLMForge |
| Use ternary weights or simpler arithmetic | BitNet, MatMul-free LM, ELiTeFormer |
| Use lookup-based FPGA computation | LUT-LLM |
| Add a sparse long-term memory | Memorizing Transformers, NSA, HSA/RAMba |

FABNet is especially close: it already includes a custom memory layout to address bank conflicts and a unified computation engine. TransFusion already goes beyond attention-only fusion. A proposal saying that previous work ignores banking or whole-layer scheduling would be incorrect. See [R04](research/fpga2/RELATED_WORK.md#r04) and [R12](research/fpga2/RELATED_WORK.md#r12).

### Candidate gap worth testing

For a small **causal decoder**, can a deliberately limited cross-group communication pattern preserve quality while improving the physical Pareto frontier at a fixed on-chip memory/port budget, relative to both fused dense execution and existing structured projections?

The potentially useful contribution is the joint result:

- a precisely specified mathematical restriction with an explicit capacity/mixing argument;
- a schedule whose buffer, bank-port, and communication requirements can be derived and checked;
- matched causal language-model evaluation;
- measured FPGA results and a separately identified ASIC implementation study;
- persistence of the architecture's benefit after distillation and task fine-tuning.

Neither using a smaller FPGA nor combining several known techniques establishes novelty. If reproducing a prior structured method with a good schedule explains the entire gain, report an implementation or characterization contribution. Do not rename it as a new block.

## 3. Candidate block family and mathematical checks

This section defines **test vehicles**, not an invented or validated architecture. Existing grouped and structured methods are starting baselines.

For row-vector input `h` of width `d`, a dense gated FFN is:

```text
F(h) = (SiLU(h Wg) elementwise_mul (h Wu)) Wd
Wg, Wu: d x m; Wd: m x d
```

Ignoring biases, it contains `3*d*m` weights and approximately that many MACs per token. For ordinary GELU FFNs, use `2*d*m` and the actual graph instead. No comparisons may silently switch activation or expansion ratio.

A grouped test family is:

```text
z = h Pin
partition z into G groups z_i, each of width d/G
u_i = (SiLU(z_i Ag_i) elementwise_mul (z_i Au_i)) Ad_i
r = concatenate(u_1, ..., u_G)
F_group(h) = (r R) Pout
```

Here `Ag_i, Au_i` have shape `(d/G, m/G)` and `Ad_i` has shape `(m/G, d/G)`. Dimensions must be divisible or padding must be counted. The local gated path has `3*d*m/G` weights; the complete count includes `R`, padding, metadata, and any extra scales. `Pin` and `Pout` are fixed permutations. `R` is either identity or a small number of prescribed sparse/block mixing stages. The surrounding transformer retains its residual connections and causal attention.

Start with identity permutations as a control, then compare unrestricted fixed shuffles with schedules realizable under the target bank/port budget. A fixed permutation is not automatically physically free: it can induce wiring, bank conflicts, address logic, or a buffer transpose. A mathematical restriction on permitted mixing must be explicit in both training and hardware.

**Expressivity risk:** grouping removes connections. It may damage quality, even if the surrounding attention retains dense projections. Additional mixing can restore information paths while erasing the hardware saving. This trade-off is the experiment. Do not claim equivalence to a dense FFN or assume that full matrix rank implies equivalent nonlinear expressivity.

Compare against a second test family using established products of block-diagonal factors and permutations, with an implemented Monarch/butterfly-style baseline. Do not claim those factors as new. Low-rank/SVD and shared-projection methods are comparison points rather than a fallback novelty claim.

### Algebraic restrictions

- Adjacent linear maps can be composed only when the actual computation permits it. Activation, gating, normalization, residual addition, positional transforms, or different attention roles can prevent an apparent fusion.
- In general, `SiLU(x A) B` cannot be replaced by `SiLU(x A B)`.
- A full-rank basis change is often a reparameterization; a rank restriction changes capacity. Distinguish them.
- Concatenating independent Q/K/V matrices into one GEMM changes scheduling, not necessarily the model class.
- Changing FFN dimensions does not remove the KV cache. Claim only the costs actually changed.
- GPU training efficiency and FPGA/ASIC execution efficiency can differ. Account for both without substituting one for the other.

### First falsifiable hypothesis

At matched quality and numerical representation, constraining channel mixing to a realizable bank schedule can lower total block communication and accelerator cost enough to improve complete-model latency over strong baselines.

Reject or revise this hypothesis if lost quality requires enough extra width, depth, dense correction, or training to eliminate the measured advantage; if permutations or spills dominate; or if an optimized dense implementation performs equally well.

## 4. Cost model: count movement at each level

For each candidate, keep separate counters for:

1. off-chip weight, KV/state, activation, and metadata bytes;
2. on-chip SRAM reads and writes, including double buffers and partial sums;
3. transfers between banks or processing elements, and permutation/transpose buffers;
4. arithmetic, nonlinear operations, conversions, and control;
5. host/device transfers and CPU work in the complete system.

A useful screening bound is:

```text
time >= max(operations / sustained_compute,
            offchip_bytes / sustained_bandwidth)
```

This is a lower bound, not a latency predictor or a proof of speedup. Dependencies, limited SRAM ports, synchronization, refill bubbles, and low utilization add cost. Measure sustained bandwidth on the actual board and access pattern.

Example arithmetic: with `d=768`, `m=2048`, a dense gated FFN contains 4,718,592 weights, or 9 MiB in FP16. Four equal groups use 1,179,648 local-path weights, or 2.25 MiB, **before** added mixing and implementation overhead. These are illustrative counts, not a model-quality result, a fit guarantee, or a 4x speedup prediction.

A good fused dense FFN can already avoid writing its expanded activation to DRAM. Our baseline must use appropriate tiling and on-chip output accumulation. Savings against an intentionally unfused implementation are insufficient to establish an architectural benefit.

Use an Amdahl-style check before scaling: if the changed component occupies fraction `f` of baseline time and its speedup is `s`, an optimistic complete-system bound is `1 / ((1-f)+f/s)` when the remaining costs are unchanged. Include new overhead explicitly.

## 5. Stage 1 protocol

### 5.1 Scope and workload

Proposed initial model tier: roughly 100–150M parameters, with a smaller pilot if needed. A later 300–400M tier is conditional on measured training feasibility and an encouraging smaller result. No particular checkpoint or token budget is frozen yet.

Use a causal decoder with one tokenizer, vocabulary, context policy, normalization, attention configuration, and training corpus shared across arms. Do not use RFT's 24M-token result as evidence that this scale is sufficiently trained. Either train all pilot arms under a matched recipe or initialize all arms from the same applicable parent with matched adaptation; report the chosen regime clearly.

Suggested deployment grid: batch 1 as the primary edge workload; prompt lengths 512, 2,048, and a supported longer length; fixed generated-token count; batch 4 as a secondary point if memory permits. Freeze the grid before final evaluation. Prefill, decode, and end-to-end request latency are separate outcomes.

### 5.2 Baselines and ablations

| Arm | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Dense reference with tuned fusion/tiling | Establish the strong hardware and model baseline |
| Smaller dense FFN | Test whether ordinary width reduction explains the result |
| Established grouped FFN | Test whether grouping alone explains the result |
| Monarch/butterfly structured projection | Closest structured-computation control |
| SVD/shared-projection alternative | Test whether simpler compression delivers the same frontier |
| Proposed restricted communication pattern | Test the specific hardware-informed restriction |
| Same mathematical candidate with a conventional schedule | Separate model structure from scheduling benefit |
| Same structure with unrestricted versus constrained permutations | Measure the quality/communication trade-off |

Run a small screening set first; fully implement the surviving closest competitors. Choose these using development results under a disclosed rule, not after opening the final test set. Explain any faithful reimplementation that cannot use released code.

Use both comparable parameter/storage budgets and quality-matched Pareto comparisons. Report actual parameter count, packed bytes, training tokens, optimizer updates, accelerator time, and search cost. Equal parameters, equal data, equal FLOPs, and equal wall time are different controls; do not claim they all hold automatically.

### 5.3 Quality and correctness

- Held-out general-text loss/perplexity on a pinned corpus and evaluation policy. Compare perplexity only with the same tokenizer, document boundaries, context, and stride.
- A small pinned suite of language understanding/reasoning tasks appropriate to model scale; later include code/RTL tasks. Guard against all models sitting at floor.
- At least three independent training seeds for the finalist comparison where feasible. Match data order/seed pairing; report seed dispersion separately from example-level uncertainty. Three seeds still provide limited precision.
- Golden operator tests for numeric error, overflow, masking, state reset, and prefill/decode agreement. Hardware implements the changed model faithfully; it need not be equivalent to the original dense model.
- A fixed-point numerical contract: bit widths, accumulator range, rounding, saturation, scaling, and nonlinear approximation. Evaluate exported hardware arithmetic, not only fake-quantized GPU behavior.

Use practical non-inferiority margins for quality rather than interpreting a nonsignificant difference as proof of equality. Possible planning margins are 0.05 nats/token in loss and 2 percentage points on a specified aggregate task score, but these are **draft engineering criteria**, not preregistered or universally appropriate thresholds. Choose and justify final margins before confirmatory evaluation.

### 5.4 Physical evaluation and stage gate

Measure the entire FFN first, then the complete transformer block, then the complete decoder. Microkernel speed alone does not establish model acceleration. Report bytes/counters and stalls alongside latency to explain the mechanism.

FPGA evidence: identical device/tool/constraints, resources, memory ports, achieved period, cycles, prefill/decode latency, and explicit timing coverage. Apply constraints before synthesis and check setup, hold, pulse width, and unconstrained paths. Include DMA, host operations, and model-loading costs according to a declared cold/warm boundary.

ASIC evidence: same library/PDK, voltage/corner, SRAM organization, tool version, utilization target, timing constraints, and interface model for every arm. Report post-route area and timing, routing/congestion evidence, and activity-based estimated power when available. If SRAM macros are unavailable, separate memory estimates from logic and identify register-mapped memory; do not silently present it as a realistic SRAM chip. [OpenROAD](https://github.com/The-OpenROAD-Project/OpenROAD) is a possible flow, not an installed or validated resource in this project.

Stage 1 advances only after a finalist meets the declared quality margin and improves the hardware Pareto frontier against the strongest feasible baselines, with a mechanistic explanation and no hidden area/latency violation. A useful pilot target could be 20% less measured traffic and 10% lower latency at no higher allowed area, but targets are provisional and not promised outcomes. If only traffic improves, report that outcome without upgrading it to latency or energy improvement.

## 6. Stage 2: distillation, adaptation, physical-reward training

### 6.1 Distill into the fixed architecture

Freeze the selected student graph before the main distillation comparison. Choose a teacher that can run on available hardware and offers useful language/code competence. RTLCoder and Qwen2.5-Coder-7B were recorded as available in the previous project; checkpoint identity, license, tokenizer, and actual files must be checked before selection.

Start with ordinary next-token loss plus teacher-distribution distillation when vocabularies/tokenizers align. Teacher-generated sequences are another option. Different tokenizers require explicit alignment or sequence-level distillation; a KL loss over incompatible vocabulary coordinates is invalid. Intermediate feature matching is optional and requires adapters for incompatible dimensions.

MiniLLM and Minitron are prior methods, not novelty claims for FPGA2. See [R26–R28](research/fpga2/RELATED_WORK.md#r26). Budget teacher inference, logit storage, and student training separately. Do not assume storing full-vocabulary logits for an entire corpus is inexpensive; choose online or documented compressed targets based on measurement.

Both the dense and structured students receive matched teacher/data access and tuning effort. Preserve pre-distillation checkpoints to measure how much recovery distillation supplies.

### 6.2 Preserve the executable structure during adaptation

Fine-tuning must stay within the selected sparsity/factorization constraints. A generic dense LoRA residual can introduce unsupported connections; merging it into a structured matrix may destroy that structure. Update permitted factors or use an explicitly supported adapter whose runtime and storage are counted. Re-export and remeasure after distillation, SFT, and policy training, including any quantization recalibration.

### 6.3 Apply the submitted paper's method to RTL generation

Sequence: teacher distillation -> oracle-verified RTL SFT -> physical-reward policy training -> frozen held-out evaluation. Keep the SFT checkpoint as the reference. The paper uses a group-relative, length-normalized update with a reference regularizer; it explicitly does **not** implement the standard clipped GRPO objective or an unbiased sequence-KL estimator. Reuse the documented method accurately or label any new objective as a changed method.

Use a functional gate and independent physical evaluation of generated circuits. Prefer a constrained utility reflecting the intended circuit workload; the earlier frequency-only endpoint is not automatically appropriate. Preserve failure accounting and equal draw budgets, and additionally report total training/generation/oracle/EDA cost. Do not reuse the FIR-trained RF predictor as a general predictor of transformer accelerator cost.

Required controls include distilled student before policy training, correctness-only training, physical-reward training, and a dense student with the same Stage 2 recipe. Sample selection/best-of-N must have matched budgets and account for the cost of physical selection.

Report two panels: **student inference PPA/latency** and **generated RTL utility/correctness**. Also retain general-text and general-code checks to detect specialization damage. Physical reward training can increase circuit utility while decreasing functional yield; an aggregate gain must not hide that trade-off.

## 7. RFT-LM: bounded reuse decision

**Decision now: keep the external memory/router out of the core Stage 1 architecture. Reuse its evaluation lessons and, after code audit, any applicable training/evaluation infrastructure.** This follows the supervisor's focus and prevents long-context memory retrieval from becoming an additional uncontrolled architectural change.

The user reported a 125M model with local attention, FIFO memory, supervised top-64 routing, and a trainable readout; NIAH improved while held-out C4 perplexity remained comparable. These are user-reported prior-project results, not independently reproduced FPGA2 evidence. Repository, checkpoints, licensing, exact splits, and matching conditions have not been inspected.

Useful lessons to carry forward:

- Always measure language-model quality alongside a targeted task; the earlier NIAH/perplexity mismatch is a strong warning against single-benchmark selection.
- Preserve ablations and negative results. Do not carry an auxiliary loss simply because it sounded central to the design.
- Separate retrieval recall from successful use of retrieved information.
- A disabled-memory ablation shows reliance within the tested checkpoint, not superiority over all full-context alternatives.
- Test the suspected chunk-boundary mechanism by varying chunk alignment independently of needle position.

Reconsider the memory operator only if profiling establishes a long-context memory bottleneck that the Stage 1 block cannot address. Then compare it independently with local attention, full/GQA attention, and relevant sparse-memory baselines. Count the router's full search, top-k selection, random reads, writes, metadata, and memory lifetime. Sparse output does not imply sparse search.

No router supervision may leak evaluation answers or future tokens into inference. Read-before-write ordering, FIFO reset between documents, and train/test key distributions require auditing. The reported failure to graft onto a frozen decoder limits that tested recipe; it is not a universal impossibility result.

The previous master reference records coarse VCCINT telemetry on both boards. Validate sustained workload resolution before making energy a primary measured outcome; otherwise use suitable external measurement or clearly labeled activity-based estimates. Neither zero DSP usage nor higher clock frequency proves lower energy.
